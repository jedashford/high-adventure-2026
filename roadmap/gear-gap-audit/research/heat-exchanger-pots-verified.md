# Heat-exchanger pots — verification pass (2026-07-29)

Triggered by a finding in the stove audit: the guide had begun telling readers "the heat
exchanger is what decides your fuel," while every pot in the guide was plain titanium or
aluminium. There was no way to act on the advice except buying a complete Jetboil system.

**Headline finding: you cannot buy the good pots separately.** The obvious move — put a
Jetboil FluxRing pot on a cheap burner — is blocked by the manufacturers themselves.

## The proprietary wall

**MSR WindBurner pots — PROPRIETARY.** MSR's own FAQ, verbatim: *"WindBurner pots must be
used only with WindBurner stoves. This is because the pot and stove are specifically
engineered to create an integrated system."* Also *"only WindBurner pots may be used on
WindBurner stoves."* The 4.5 L WindBurner Stock Pot ($129.95) is explicitly *"not for use
with the WindBurner Personal top-mounted stove"* either.
Source: https://cascadedesigns.com/products/windburner-duo-stove-system ,
https://cascadedesigns.com/products/windburner-r-stock-pot

**Jetboil FluxRing pots — PROPRIETARY.** The 1.5 L Ceramic Cook Pot ($74.99, 14.4 oz) IS
sold as a standalone accessory, but its compatibility list is twelve Jetboil burners and
nothing else: *"Flash, Flash 1.0L, Flash 1.8L, Luna, MicroMo, MightyMo, MiniMo, SUMO,
TrailCook 1.2L, TrailCook 2.0L, Zip, Zip 0.8L."* No third-party burner appears anywhere.
The 5 L Genesis FluxPot is even narrower — *"Not compatible with backpacking stoves: Zip,
Flash, TrailCook, MightyMo, MicroMo, MiniMo, SUMO, Joule."*
Note the reverse IS true: the TrailCook burner accepts *"3rd party cookware up to 9" and 2L
capacity"* — you can put a plain pot on a Jetboil burner, not a Jetboil pot on a plain one.
Source: https://jetboil.johnsonoutdoors.com/us/shop/cookware/pots-pans/15l-ceramic-cook-pot ,
.../cookware/pots-pans/5l-cook-pot-lid , .../parts-accessories/cooking-accessories/pot-support

This is why Adventure Alan had to buy a whole $135 Stash system to get its pot. There was
no other way.

## The group-size gap — no good answer exists

Searched specifically for a 1.5-2.5 L heat-exchanger pot that fits a normal canister burner,
so one stove could feed four in a single boil. **There isn't one.**

- **Primus ETA series** — gone. Primus's current pots-and-pans collection lists only
  CampFire, PrimeTech, Essential and LiTech. No ETA product. Retailers list old stock only.
- **Optimus Terra Xpress HE** — 1.75 L, 380 g, $44.95, real heat exchanger (*"Built in
  heat-exchanger on the bottom reduces boiling time by 30%"*). Perfect spec match, but
  **discontinued** — GOHUNT says "Sorry, this product has been discontinued," Gear X out of
  stock, no live add-to-cart found anywhere.
- **Primus PrimeTech 2.3 L** — $129.99, real heat exchanger, **in stock**, but the page says
  *"Optimised for all of our stoves"* (Primus's own). No proprietary lock is described, so it
  likely sits on any upright burner, but cross-brand fit is NOT confirmed. PARTIAL.
- **Fire-Maple Feast HX Set** — the only Fire-Maple product with a 1.5 L heat-exchanger pot,
  and it only ships as a $69.95 three-piece set: *"Both the 1.5 L pot and 0.7 L kettle have
  integrated heat-exchanger bases. The frypan has a conventional flat base and does not
  include a heat exchanger."* Standard-burner fit not stated either way.
- **MSR clip-on Heat Exchanger accessory** — $89.95, 6 oz, *"clip-on efficiency booster for
  MSR pots"*, fits 6.6-7.3 in pots. But it is specified to work best *"in conjunction with
  the Windscreen and Heat Reflector when used with an MSR liquid fuel or remote-burner
  canister stove"* — not confirmed for a bare canister-top burner. Out of stock.

Every Fire-Maple **standalone** HX pot tops out at 1 L. Alocs and Widesea sell 1 L HX pots

## GSI has no heat-exchanger pot

Pulled GSI's full 356-product catalogue via `products.json?limit=250` (2 pages) and keyword
scanned for "flux", "heat exchanger", "fin", "heat sink". **Zero products use "flux", "heat
exchanger" or "fin".** The Halulite Boiler is CONFIRMED plain aluminium — matching the
earlier cookware audit — at $49.95 (1.1 L) / $54.95 (1.8 L), both `available: true`.
GSI's "HS" line advertises a *"hard-anodized heat-sink base"* claimed to cook 30% faster,
which is **not** the same as a flux ring; a manufacturer photo of the Escape HS 2 L base
shows a flat anodized disc, not radiating fins. Do not list any GSI pot as a heat exchanger.

## Why this matters less than it looked

The whole heat-exchanger argument was driven by the 12-person group requirement of 48-56 L
of boiling. **At two-person scale it collapses.** Three dinners plus three breakfasts for
two people is roughly 8 L. Even the least efficient combination in the guide — a bare burner
with a plain pot, 16 L per 230 g canister — finishes the trip on 63% of one canister. The
heat exchanger saves perhaps 50 g of fuel over four days.

So the pots above are listed as the honest cheap route to efficiency for anyone outfitting
the group stoves, but the duo builds do **not** lean on them for fuel reasons. There the 1 L
capacity is the point: one boil covers both freeze-dried pouches instead of two.

## Titanium vs aluminium — and a sizing error this caught

**No titanium heat-exchanger pot exists, and none will.** Titanium conducts heat at roughly
**1/11th** the rate of aluminium (~22 vs ~237 W/m·K). A fin ring only helps if heat conducts
from the fin through the pot wall into the water; in titanium it would not. That is why every
heat-exchanger pot verified in this pass — Jetboil FluxRing, MSR WindBurner/Reactor, Olicamp
XTS, Fire-Maple — is aluminium, and why TOAKS, a titanium specialist, makes none. Their own
site search for "heat exchanger" returns zero results.

**Titanium's weakness does not apply to this trip.** Poor conduction creates scorching hot
spots, which ruins real cooking. It is irrelevant for boiling, because water convects and
self-equalises. `packcheck.html` specifies "boiling for freeze-dried meals, so no cooking and
no cleanup" — so the one drawback never bites.

**Combined with the duo-scale fuel finding, titanium is simply the better choice for two.**
One 230 g canister covers the trip on any setup, so the heat exchanger buys nothing here, and
the titanium pot is lighter and larger:

| | Price | Weight | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire-Maple XK6 (aluminium, HX) | $29.95 | 6.7 oz | 1.0 L |
| TOAKS 1300 ml BH (titanium) | $52.95 | **5 oz** | **1.3 L** |

**Sizing error this caught.** The Best Value duo build initially read "1 L does both pouches in
one boil." That is false. Two freeze-dried pouches need up to 2 cups each, about 950 ml. In a
1.0 L pot that is brim-full and cannot reach a rolling boil without spilling, so a 1 L pot
still means two boils. **1.3 L is the smallest size that genuinely does two pouches at once.**
Corrected 2026-07-29; the build now specifies the TOAKS 1300 ml BH.

The general lesson: for a duo boiling water, **headroom matters more than fins**. Capacity is
the spec that removes a boil; the heat exchanger only removes fuel weight, which is already a
non-issue at this scale.

The Fire-Maple XK6 and Olicamp XTS remain listed in Cookware — they are still the cheapest
verified route to heat-exchanger efficiency for anyone outfitting the 12-person group stoves,
where the 48-56 L requirement makes fuel efficiency real.
