# One-Pot Stove Dinners & Breakfasts — Verified Recipes

Compiled 2026-08-01 for the Sawtooth trip (Aug 10–13). Scope: **one-pot canister-stove
dinners and breakfasts only** (frypan meals, fresh food, and no-cook lunches are covered
separately). Kit assumed throughout: Toaks Titanium 1650 ml pot, upright canister stove,
pot cozy (or a jacket wrapped around the lidded pot), food hang, no refrigeration.

**Verification:** every Source URL below was opened and read on 2026-08-01. Ingredient
quantities are quoted as the source states them. In-camp steps are paraphrased.
Calories/protein appear only where the source states them — nothing is computed or
estimated here. Anything not from a published source is explicitly labeled
**[our build / our note]**.

**Ratings:** **top shelf** = make these first (easy, teen-proof, fuel-cheap) ·
**solid** = works, minor fuss · **leader treat** = adults will love it, teens may not ·
**demo only** = fun once, not for feeding the group.

## Ramen builds

### 1. Ramen Bomb — the thru-hiker classic

Ramen + instant mashed potatoes: the potatoes drink the ramen water, so nothing gets
drained and everything gets eaten.

- **Source:** https://hike.guide/ramen-bomb/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (as stated — the source is deliberately loose):** 1–2 packets ramen
  (usually one seasoning sachet is enough) · instant mashed potatoes, about ¼–½ of a
  pouch · Spam, tuna, or another foil-pack protein. Add-ins the source endorses:
  Cheetos, chips, cheese.
- **Servings:** not stated. **[Our note]** one 2-packet build with ½ pouch potatoes and a
  protein pouch is one very hungry teen's dinner.
- **At-home prep:** none required; pre-portion the potato flakes into a zip bag if you're
  splitting a big Idahoan pouch.
- **In-camp:** boil the ramen in generous water with its seasoning packet (source: extra
  water is better — do not drain). After a few minutes, when noodles are done, kill the
  flame and stir in the potato flakes; they absorb the remaining water almost
  immediately. Stir in the protein and any add-ins.
- **Calories/protein:** not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~5 minutes of flame total; zero drain water; pot wipes clean because
  the starch absorbs everything. One of the best fuel-per-calorie meals in this book.
- **Scaling for 2–3 teens:** **[our build — common-practice ratios, not from a published
  recipe]** 3–4 ramen packets + 1 full 4-serving Idahoan pouch + 2 protein pouches fills
  the 1650 ml Toaks to a sane level and feeds 2–3 teens; use 2 seasoning sachets.
- **Rating: top shelf** — it's ramen; nobody has to be talked into it, and it can't
  really be ruined.

### 2. Jerky Ramen

Ramen upgraded into a brothy beef-noodle soup with jerky that softens in the hot broth.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/jerky-ramen/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** 1 packet dashi powder (5 g) **or** 1 tsp vegetable
  bouillon powder · ¼ cup dehydrated frozen-vegetable mix · 1 block ramen noodles
  (~50 g) · 1 pack beef jerky (25 g).
- **At-home prep:** none required per the source; bag the dry goods together.
- **In-camp:** stir the dashi/bouillon and vegetables into 1½ cups water, bring to a
  boil, then simmer low ~5 minutes. Add the ramen and jerky and cook another 3–4 minutes
  until the noodles are done.
- **Calories/protein:** 388 cal, 17.6 g protein per serving (stated).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~8–9 min of flame — mid-pack; brothy, so boys drink the pot clean
  (bear-smart: make sure they finish it). **Sourcing flag:** dashi is a specialty item —
  the page itself offers the bouillon swap; the dehydrated vegetable mix is
  online/dehydrator territory, and **[our note]** the meal works without it.
- **Scaling:** triple everything in the 1650 ml pot; 388 cal is light for a teen dinner,
  so run at least 1.5 blocks of ramen per boy.
- **Rating: solid** — teen-proof flavors; only the specialty broth/veg sourcing keeps it
  off the top shelf.

### 3. Spicy Peanut Butter Ramen

Ramen with a five-ingredient peanut sauce mixed at home — pad-thai energy, no draining.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/spicy-peanut-butter-ramen/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** ¼ cup dehydrated frozen-vegetable mix · 1 block ramen
  noodles (~50 g / 1.7 oz) · sauce: 1 tbsp peanut butter, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp rice
  vinegar, 1 tsp sriracha, 1 clove garlic (minced).
- **At-home prep:** mix the sauce ingredients in a mug and decant into a small leakproof
  bottle. Pack everything else separately.
- **In-camp:** stir vegetables into ⅔ cup water, boil, simmer low ~5 minutes. Add ramen
  and cook 3–4 minutes until tender (the low water mostly absorbs). Off flame, stir the
  sauce through.
- **Calories/protein:** 463 cal, 14.9 g protein per serving (stated).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~8 min flame; near-zero liquid left, peanut film rinses with a hot
  swish. Peanut-allergy check before the trip. Sriracha is adjustable per bag —
  **[our note]** halve it for younger teens. Vegetable mix is optional in practice.
- **Scaling:** sauce scales linearly; pre-mix a triple batch in one 4 oz bottle for a
  3-teen pot.
- **Rating: solid** — a hit with adventurous kids; sauce bottle is the only fuss.

### 4. Revamped Ramen (soba & shiitake)

A grown-up ramen: soba noodles, shiitake, jerky, and a soy-sesame-sriracha finisher.

- **Source:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/backpacking-revamped-ramen/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** 3.5 oz buckwheat soba noodles, broken in half · ½ cup
  freeze-dried vegetables · 2 oz jerky · 4 dried shiitake mushrooms, quartered · ½ tsp
  salt · sauce: 1 tbsp soy sauce or liquid aminos, 1 tsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tsp
  sriracha.
- **At-home prep:** noodles, vegetables, mushrooms, salt, and the jerky go in one zip
  bag; combine the three sauce ingredients in a small sealed container.
- **In-camp:** add the dry bag to 2 cups (16 oz) water, bring to a boil, cook ~5 minutes
  until noodles are tender. Off heat, stir in jerky and the sauce.
- **Calories/protein:** 688 cal per serving stated; protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** one boil + 5 min — good; brothy pot to finish. **Sourcing flag:**
  freeze-dried vegetables and dried shiitake are online/health-food purchases (the page
  points to Amazon/REI/Whole Foods).
- **Scaling:** 2 servings fills the Toaks; run two rounds for 3 teens.
- **Rating: leader treat** — mushrooms and soba are a gamble with picky 14-year-olds;
  adults will be delighted.

## Peanut & teriyaki noodle bowls

### 5. Chili Lime Peanut Noodles

FOTG's quantified pad-thai-ish ramen — sweet, limey, crunchy peanuts.

- **Source:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/backpacking-chili-lime-peanut-noodles/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** 3 oz ramen noodles (one packet, seasoning discarded) ·
  1 tbsp brown sugar · ¼ tsp ground ginger · ¼ tsp red pepper flakes · 1 tbsp chopped
  peanuts · 1 peanut butter packet (2 tbsp) · 1 packet True Lime powder.
- **At-home prep:** noodles, brown sugar, ginger, pepper flakes, and peanuts in one bag;
  PB packet and True Lime ride alongside.
- **In-camp:** water to just barely cover the noodles; boil a few minutes until done.
  Off heat, pour off all but about ¼ cup of water, then stir in the spice bag, peanut
  butter, and True Lime until creamy.
- **Calories/protein:** 716 cal, 18 g protein per serving (stated; also 35 g fat, 86 g
  carbs).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** one short boil; the only meal in this book with a drain step — pour
  the ~few tablespoons of excess into a wide arc well away from camp, or start with less
  water. Peanut-allergy check. Pepper flakes per-bag adjustable.
- **Scaling:** everything is per-packet, so scale per boy; a 3-packet batch fits the
  Toaks.
- **Rating: solid** — 716 stated calories per ramen packet is excellent; allergy check
  and the mini-drain keep it from top shelf.

### 6. Peanut Noodles (Skurka)

The guided-trip crowd favorite — ramen tossed in a home-mixed peanut sauce, no simmer.

- **Source:** https://andrewskurka.com/backpacking-dinner-recipe-peanut-noodles/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (identities verified on page):** ramen noodles (flavor packet discarded) ·
  crushed peanuts, cashews, or raisins · sauce of peanut butter, soy sauce, garlic,
  ginger, lime juice, toasted sesame oil, olive oil, honey.
  **Quantity caveat:** the exact per-serving amounts live in an embedded Google Sheet on
  the page that could not be captured by our fetcher — open the live page's spreadsheet
  before shopping. Stated meal stats: recommended meal weight 5.7 oz.
- **At-home prep:** the sauce is made entirely at home — mix everything except noodles
  and nuts, and carry it in a 4 or 8 oz wide-mouth HDPE bottle (the page warns 2 oz jars
  are miserable to pour from). Optionally crush the noodles in the wrapper first.
- **In-camp:** boil at least 8 oz water, add the ramen and nuts, kill the stove, and let
  it sit 5 minutes. Stir the sauce through (it can go in any time).
- **Calories/protein:** 809 cal, 141 cal/oz per serving (stated); protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** boil-and-soak — among the most fuel-cheap dinners here; peanut-oily
  pot wants a hot-water swish. Gluten-free swap (rice ramen) and alternative nut butters
  are on the page.
- **Scaling:** Skurka runs this for client groups; pre-mix one big sauce bottle and give
  each boy his own noodle ration.
- **Rating: solid** — 809 cal of teen-friendly noodles with no simmer; only the
  quantity-behind-a-spreadsheet problem keeps it from top shelf until the leader pulls
  the sheet.

## Rice & beans / rice bowls

### 7. Beans + Rice with Fritos & Cheese

Skurka's most-requested client dinner — a walking taco in a pot, 60 seconds of simmer.

- **Source:** https://andrewskurka.com/backpacking-dinner-recipe-beans-rice-with-fritos-cheese/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (per serving):** 2 oz instant refried beans · 1.5 oz instant rice ·
  1 oz cheddar cheese · 1 oz Fritos · 0.2 oz (≈2 tsp) taco seasoning · at least 10 oz
  water. Recommended meal weight 5.7 oz.
- **At-home prep:** beans + rice + taco seasoning can be pre-bagged (for groups, one
  3.5 oz snack bag of the bean/rice mix per person); keep cheese separate. Bean/rice
  bags keep 6–9 months stored cool and dry (stated).
- **In-camp:** bring the water to a boil **first** (adding solids early scorches), pour
  in the bean/rice/seasoning bag, and simmer only ~60 seconds, stirring. Off heat, stir
  in diced cheese; add Fritos at the last second so they stay crunchy.
- **Calories/protein:** 668 cal, 117 cal/oz, 27 g protein per serving (stated).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~5 minutes total flame, everything absorbs — no drain; the cheese
  film wants one hot-water swish. Best fuel-to-calories ratio of any dinner here.
- **Scaling:** three bagged servings cook fine together in the Toaks with ~30 oz water.
  **[Our note]** serve in tortillas for burritos — same recipe, double the enthusiasm.
- **Rating: top shelf** — Skurka's own favorite, quantified to the tenth of an ounce,
  and teens recognize every ingredient.

### 8. Chicken and Rice

Greenbelly's four-ingredient comfort classic: instant rice, chicken pouch, soy, oil.

- **Source:** https://www.greenbelly.co/pages/best-backpacking-meals [opened]
- **Ingredients (as stated):** pre-cooked chicken 100 g · instant rice 80 g · dried
  broccoli 35 g · olive oil 20 g · soy sauce 3 g.
- **At-home prep:** bag rice and broccoli; chicken pouch, oil, and soy travel as-is.
- **In-camp:** boil water at a 2:1 water-to-dry ratio; add broccoli for 5 minutes, then
  rice for 5 minutes, then stir in chicken, soy, and oil off the heat.
- **Calories/protein:** not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~10 min flame as written — cut it by using the cozy (boil 2 min,
  wrap 10). Absorb-style, no drain. **[Our note]** dried broccoli is a specialty buy;
  the meal is complete without it.
- **Scaling:** double the grams for 2 teens, and the olive oil is the cheapest way to
  add calories (250 cal/oz).
- **Rating: top shelf** — plainest, safest dinner in the book; nobody has ever rejected
  chicken and rice.

### 9. Teriyaki Salmon Rice Bowl

Instant rice + salmon pouch + teriyaki drizzle + nori — a five-minute rice bowl.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/teriyaki-salmon-rice-bowl/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** ⅓ cup dehydrated or instant rice · 1 tbsp nori, cut in
  thin strips · 1 tbsp teriyaki sauce · 1 pouch/can boneless pink salmon (~70 g /
  2.47 oz) · 1 tsp sesame seeds (optional).
- **At-home prep:** rice in a small freezer zip bag; teriyaki in a small leakproof
  bottle; the rest packs as-is.
- **In-camp:** rice + ⅓ cup water, boil ~5 minutes stirring until rehydrated (or per
  instant-rice package), drain any excess. Stir in nori, top with salmon, drizzle
  teriyaki, sprinkle sesame.
- **Calories/protein:** 353 cal, 19.8 g protein per serving (stated).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** one 5-min boil; near-dry pot. Salmon pouch is the smelliest trash of
  the trip — double-bag it for the hang.
- **Scaling:** 353 cal is a light dinner — run 2–3x per teen, which still fits the pot.
- **Rating: solid** — dead simple, but fish is a coin-flip with teens; poll the group
  before buying six salmon pouches.

## Couscous dinners

*Couscous is the fuel cheat code: it never sits on the flame, so it cannot scorch the
titanium pot. Boil, stir, lid on, wait 5.*

### 10. Sun-Dried Tomato Couscous

Backcountry Foodie's dietitian-written, all-grocery couscous — pizza-adjacent flavors.

- **Source:** https://backcountryfoodie.com/sun-dried-tomato-couscous-ultralight-backpacking-recipe/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** ½ cup (86 g) couscous · 3 pieces (15 g) sun-dried
  tomatoes, diced · 2 tbsp (20 g) pine nuts · ½ tsp each dried parsley, oregano, basil,
  turmeric · ½ tsp (1.5 g) garlic powder · ¼ tsp black pepper · ⅛ tsp salt · 1 tbsp
  (14 g) olive oil · 6 oz (180 mL) water.
- **At-home prep:** all dry ingredients in one bag; olive oil in a leakproof container.
- **In-camp:** add 6 oz hot water to the bag or pot, stir, stand a few minutes until
  absorbed (the page also blesses a 30–45 min cold soak). Stir in the olive oil.
- **Calories/protein:** 636 cal, 18 g protein, 127 cal/oz per serving (stated).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** boil-only — top-tier fuel economy; if rehydrated in the bag, the pot
  never even gets dirty. Page confirms all ingredients are grocery-store.
- **Scaling:** **[our note]** add a 7 oz chicken pouch per two servings to make it a
  teen main; pine nuts are pricey — the page's structure tolerates swapping in slivered
  almonds, but that's our swap, not theirs.
- **Rating: solid** — meatless as written and slightly herby for young palates, but
  bulletproof method and honest numbers.

### 11. Mediterranean Couscous

Greenbelly's four-ingredient couscous: parmesan, pesto mix, olives.

- **Source:** https://www.greenbelly.co/pages/best-backpacking-meals [opened]
- **Ingredients (as stated):** couscous 85 g · parmesan 35 g · dry pesto mix 20 g ·
  olives 20 g.
- **At-home prep:** bag couscous + pesto powder; parmesan and olives separate.
- **In-camp:** boil water at the stated 3:1 ratio, add couscous 5–10 minutes off the
  boil, stir in pesto, parmesan, olives.
- **Calories/protein:** not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** boil-only. **[Our note]** 3:1 water is on the wet side for couscous —
  err toward less water on a canister stove, since extra liquid is wasted fuel.
- **Scaling:** triple the grams for 2–3 teens; shelf-stable olive pouches (Trader Joe's)
  survive the hang fine.
- **Rating: solid** — quick and cheap; olives split the room, so let each kid opt in.

### 12. Moroccan Chicken Couscous

FOTG's apricot-almond couscous with ras el hanout — the best-documented couscous dinner.

- **Source:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/backpackers-moroccan-chicken-couscous/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (2 servings):** ½ cup couscous · 1 cup dried apricots, chopped · ¼ cup
  sliced almonds · 1 tsp sea salt · 4 tsp ras el hanout · 2 olive oil packets
  (~2 tbsp) · 1 (7 oz) packet cooked chicken · 1 packet True Lemon · 5 oz water.
- **At-home prep:** couscous, apricots, almonds, spice, and salt in one resealable bag;
  oil packets, chicken pouch, and True Lemon alongside.
- **In-camp:** boil the 5 oz water with the oil, stir in the dry bag and True Lemon, add
  chicken, lid on, off heat, stand 5 minutes. Fluff.
- **Calories/protein:** 665 cal, 40 g protein per serving (stated) — the highest stated
  protein of any dinner in this book.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** 5 oz boil is the smallest of any dinner here; zero simmer; pot
  barely gets dirty.
- **Scaling:** double batch fits the Toaks easily (it's only 10 oz water).
- **Rating: leader treat** — dried apricots and North African spice are exactly the
  dinner a 14-year-old trades away; leaders should absolutely make it for themselves.

### 13. Chicken Marbella

FOTG's trail version of the Silver Palate classic — prunes, olives, brown sugar, oregano.

- **Source:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/backpackers-chicken-marbella/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (2 servings):** ½ cup couscous · ½ cup dried prunes, chopped · 1 tbsp
  dried oregano · 1 tbsp brown sugar · ½ tsp salt · ¼ tsp garlic powder · 1 broth
  packet · 1 tbsp olive oil (or packet) · 1 (7 oz) chicken pouch · 1 (2.5 oz) pouch
  pitted green olives · 2 vinegar packets (optional).
- **At-home prep:** couscous, prunes, oregano, sugar, salt, garlic powder in one small
  zip bag; chicken, olives, oil, broth, vinegar packed separately.
- **In-camp:** boil ½ cup (4 oz) water; stir in broth packet, oil, the couscous bag, and
  chicken; off heat, covered, 5 minutes. Finish with olives and vinegar.
- **Calories/protein:** 545 cal per serving (stated); protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** 4 oz boil + zero simmer — as fuel-cheap as dinner gets.
- **Scaling:** double batch in the Toaks; olive-oil packet count is the calorie lever.
- **Rating: leader treat** — prunes *and* olives is a two-strike count with teen boys;
  it is genuinely delicious for the adults.

## Pasta & mac builds

### 14. Chili Mac and Cheese

Greenbelly's macaroni + beans + cheddar + chili powder — the teen-format chili mac.

- **Source:** https://www.greenbelly.co/pages/best-backpacking-meals [opened]
- **Ingredients (as stated):** dried beans 80 g · raw macaroni noodles 80 g · cheddar
  cheese 80 g · chili powder 10 g.
- **At-home prep:** bag beans and macaroni separately (they enter at different times);
  cube the cheddar at home to save a camp knife step.
- **In-camp:** boil water at a 2:1 ratio; beans in for 10 minutes, macaroni in for 5
  more, then off heat stir in cheddar and chili powder.
- **Calories/protein:** not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~15 min of flame as written — the fuel-hungriest dinner in this
  book. **[Our note]** using instant refried or dehydrated pre-cooked beans (never raw
  dry beans) collapses the bean step to near zero, and the pot-cozy method (boil the
  pasta 2 min, wrap 10) rescues the rest. Cheese pot needs a proper hot-water swish
  scattered away from camp.
- **Scaling:** double batch is a full Toaks; stir constantly near the end — titanium
  scorches cheese fast.
- **Rating: solid** — flavors are a guaranteed teen win; the simmer time is the tax.

### 15. Good Old Mac N Cheese

trail.recipes' scratch mac with a real cheese-sauce ratio — no neon powder packet.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/good-old-mac-n-cheese/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** 1 tbsp heavy cream powder (whole-milk powder like Nido
  works, per page) · 3 tbsp cheddar cheese powder · ½ tsp cornstarch · ⅔ cup pre-cooked
  and dehydrated pasta · salt · 1 tbsp ghee.
- **At-home prep:** whisk cream powder + cheese powder + cornstarch into one sauce bag.
  Pasta is cooked and dehydrated at home per the site's method.
- **In-camp:** pasta + ⅓ cup water, boil, then medium heat ~5 minutes stirring until
  rehydrated. Off heat, stir in the sauce bag, then the ghee until melted.
- **Calories/protein:** 656 cal, 19.1 g protein per serving (stated).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** ~6–7 min flame; low water means no drain. **Sourcing flag:** as
  written this needs home-dehydrated pasta and cheddar cheese powder (online buy) — the
  fussiest shopping list in this book. Ghee is grocery-store and needs no fridge.
- **Scaling:** the 3:1:½ sauce ratio (cheese powder : cream powder : tsp cornstarch)
  scales linearly — pre-bag one sauce bag per boy.
- **Rating: solid** — best-tasting mac formula here, but only worth it if the leader
  wants to prep pasta at home; otherwise make #14.

### 16. Pesto Noodles (Skurka)

Ramen + pesto flavors: parmesan, sun-dried tomato, basil, olive oil. No simmer at all.

- **Source:** https://andrewskurka.com/backpacking-dinner-recipe-pesto-noodles/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving; amounts as posted in the page's own comments section — the
  main table is an embedded spreadsheet):** instant/ramen noodles 3.00 oz · garlic
  powder 0.05 oz · dried basil 0.05 oz · parmesan 0.80 oz · sun-dried tomatoes 0.80 oz ·
  olive oil 1.00 oz. Total meal weight 5.6 oz (stated). ~10 oz water.
- **At-home prep:** bag everything together per serving **except** parmesan (own bag);
  olive oil in an HDPE Nalgene bottle. For groups: each boy gets a noodle ration;
  parmesan/oil/tomatoes/spices are shared containers.
- **In-camp:** get the water boiling or near it, drop in everything but the parmesan
  (ramen seasoning discarded; tomatoes benefit from going in first), stir, and let it
  reconstitute — the page is explicit that no simmer is needed. Off stove, stir in
  parmesan; salt/pepper/red-pepper to taste.
- **Calories/protein:** 700 cal, 125 cal/oz per serving (stated); protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** boil-and-soak; slightly oily pot, quick swish. Page suggests salami
  chunks, jerky, or nuts as add-ins and a vegan parmesan swap.
- **Scaling:** three rations + shared toppings is the intended group pattern.
- **Rating: solid** — fuel-cheap and hearty; basil-and-sun-dried-tomato is mildly
  adventurous for teens but rarely refused once it smells like pizza.

## Instant-potato bowls

### 17. 3 Cheese Mashed Potatoes with Kabanos

Loaded instant mash rehydrated in its own bag — the pot only ever touches water.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/3-cheese-mashed-potatoes-with-kabanos/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** ½ cup instant mashed potatoes · 1 tbsp freeze-dried
  cheddar, powdered · 1 tbsp freeze-dried grated parmesan · 1 tbsp freeze-dried
  mozzarella, powdered · 1 tbsp crispy fried onions (optional) · 4 shelf-stable Kabanos
  sausages (~50 g / 1.76 oz).
- **At-home prep:** potatoes, cheese powders, and fried onions mixed in one reusable
  zip bag; sausages packed separately.
- **In-camp:** boil ⅔ cup water, pour straight into the bag, mix well, stir in sliced
  sausage. The stove runs only long enough to boil ⅔ cup.
- **Calories/protein:** 411 cal, 22.3 g protein per serving (stated; 1214 mg sodium).
- **Fuel/cleanup:** the single cheapest-fuel dinner format in this book, and *zero* pot
  cleanup — the bag is the bowl. **Sourcing flag:** freeze-dried cheese powders are an
  online buy; Kabanos lives in European delis. **[Our note]** ordinary shelf-stable
  snack sticks (Slim Jim class) and a flavored Idahoan pouch reproduce the idea from any
  grocery store — that adaptation is ours, not the source's.
- **Scaling:** one bag per boy; 411 cal is a side for a teen — pair with a soup packet
  or double the potatoes.
- **Rating: solid** — also the designated **rescue meal**: carry one spare bag per group
  for the kid whose dinner fails.

*Potato cross-reference: the Ramen Bomb (#1) is the other instant-potato dinner here.*

## Soups & stews

### 18. Backcountry Chili

Skurka's client-trip chili: instant refried beans, tomato powder, TVP, cheese, corn chips.

- **Source:** https://andrewskurka.com/dinner-recipe-backcountry-chili/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (identities verified on page):** instant refried beans · tomato powder ·
  textured vegetable protein (TVP) · spices · cheese · Fritos-class chips. Recipe weight
  5.5 oz (stated). **Quantity caveat:** exact amounts are in the page's embedded
  spreadsheet, which could not be captured — pull them from the live page before
  shopping. ~12 oz water (page allows up to 16 for easier cleanup).
- **At-home prep:** everything mixed in one bag except the cheese (own bag). Shake the
  bag before pouring — spices settle to the bottom (stated).
- **In-camp:** boil ~12 oz water, kill the flame, stir in the chili bag, lid on ~10
  minutes to rehydrate (cozy it, or simmer if impatient). Stir in the cheese.
- **Calories/protein:** 512 cal, 93 cal/oz per serving (stated); protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** boil + cozy — very cheap; a chili pot swishes clean easier than a
  cheese pot. **Sourcing flags:** tomato powder is an online buy; TVP is Bob's Red
  Mill-tier grocery. Page warns dried beef crumbles are *not* shelf-stable once opened —
  stick with TVP for this trip.
- **Scaling:** the group pattern is one bulk bag divided in the field; Frito topping
  from the lunch supply.
- **Rating: solid** — chili + chips + cheese is teen-safe; docked only for spreadsheet
  quantities and two specialty ingredients.

### 19. Milk-and-Sugar Oatmeal Base — with 5 published variations

The exact-ratio oatmeal build: bag at home, add boiling water, wait one minute.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/backpacking-food-ideas/backpacking-oatmeal-5-creative-variations/ [opened]
- **Base ingredients (1 serving):** ½ cup quick-cooking oats · 4 tbsp milk powder ·
  pinch of salt · 1 tsp sugar. **Water:** 1 cup, boiled.
- **In-camp:** pour the boiling water into the cup or bag holding the oat mix, stir,
  cover, stand 1 minute.
- **Variations (quantities only where the page states them):** Savory miso (swap milk
  powder for instant miso soup + dried mushrooms + green onions) · Coconut curry
  (2 tbsp coconut milk powder + pinch curry powder + dried spinach/pumpkin + nuts) ·
  Matcha raspberry (1 tsp matcha + freeze-dried raspberries + sweetener) · Pumpkin
  spice (1 tbsp powdered pumpkin + ½ tsp pumpkin pie spice + brown sugar) · Apple pie
  (freeze-dried apple + pinch cinnamon + walnuts + brown sugar).
- **Calories/protein:** not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** boil-only, and if built in bags/cups the pot stays clean — the whole
  patrol eats off one boil of the 1650 ml pot (~5 cups of water = 5 servings).
- **Scaling:** pre-bag 2 base bags per teen per morning; let them add trail-mix
  components. Apple pie and pumpkin spice are the teen picks; miso and matcha are not.
- **Rating: top shelf** — this is the workhorse breakfast: exact ratio, one-minute
  stand, zero cleanup.

### 20. Apricot Ginger Oatmeal

### 21. Blueberry Coconut Oatmeal

FOTG's berry-and-coconut oatmeal with hemp seeds for protein.

- **Source:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/blueberry-coconut-oatmeal/ [opened]

### 22. Tropical Breakfast Couscous

Couscous as breakfast: coconut, pineapple, almonds — boil half a cup of water, done.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/tropical-breakfast-couscous/ [opened]

  unsweetened shredded coconut · 1 tbsp sliced almonds, toasted · 2 tbsp freeze-dried
  pineapple chunks · 1 tsp coconut sugar.
- **At-home prep:** everything in one small zip bag.
- **In-camp:** boil ½ cup water, kill the flame, pour into the couscous mix, stir,

### 23. Pistachio & Raisin Breakfast Couscous

### 24. Pecan Maple Granola in a Bag

FOTG's bake-at-home granola pre-loaded with milk powder — add water, breakfast exists.

- **Source:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/pecan-maple-granola-bag/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (makes 2 servings):** ¾ cup rolled oats · ½ cup raw pecans, chopped ·
  ¼ cup unsweetened coconut · ¼ cup dried cranberries · ¼ cup maple syrup · 1 tbsp oil
  (grapeseed or coconut) · ⅛ tsp salt — plus **1 tbsp powdered milk per serving bag**.
- **At-home prep:** mix everything except milk powder, bake compactly at 325°F for
  20–25 minutes (rotate once, don't stir), cool, break into clumps, split between two
  bags with 1 tbsp powdered milk in each.
- **In-camp:** add about ⅓ cup (3 oz) water per bag and stir — no stove required at all
  (warm water dissolves some milk powders better, per page).
- **Calories/protein:** 580 cal per serving (stated); protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** potentially **zero fuel** and zero dishes — the fastest morning in
  this book, ideal for the pack-up-and-move day 4.
- **Scaling:** quadruple the bake for the group; store-bought granola + 1 tbsp milk
  powder + 3 oz water follows the same stated ratio if nobody wants to bake
  **[our note]**.
- **Rating: top shelf** — the stated granola:milk-powder:water ratio is the exact thing
  most trip leaders wish they had written down.

### 25. Camp Grits

Dirty Gourmet's backpacking-tagged grits — Southern breakfast, real cook time.

- **Source:** https://www.dirtygourmet.com/grits/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (4 servings):** 1 cup grits · 4 cups cold water · 2 tbsp butter · salt
  and pepper · optional white cheddar, bacon, and/or country ham.
- **At-home prep:** none stated; **[our note]** pre-bag the grits with salt.
- **In-camp:** stir grits into the cold water, medium-high heat, stirring occasionally
  at a low boil until thick — about 15–20 minutes. Stir in ¼ cup more water, drop to
  low, cover (another ¼ cup if needed). Finish with butter, cheese, bacon bits.
- **Calories/protein:** not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** the 15–20 minute cook makes this the most fuel-expensive breakfast
  here, and a starch-crusted pot follows. **[Our note]** instant or quick grits
  (5-minute box directions) preserve the idea at a fraction of the fuel, but that swap
  is ours — the source recipe is as written above.
- **Scaling:** the recipe already feeds 4 — one batch covers the patrol with cheese and
  bacon bits from the lunch bag.
- **Rating: solid** — cheesy grits are an easy teen sell and it's the only 4-serving
  batch recipe here, but budget the fuel or make the instant swap.

### 26. Really Hot Chocolate

A quantified DIY cocoa: real grated chocolate plus milk powder, one cup of water.

- **Source:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/really-hot-chocolate/ [opened]
- **Ingredients (1 serving):** 1 tsp cocoa powder · 2 tsp sugar (or to taste) · 10 g
  dark chocolate (70% cacao), grated · 2 tbsp full-cream milk powder · ½ tsp cornstarch ·
  pinch of salt · pinch of cayenne.
- **At-home prep:** everything into one small zip freezer bag per mug.
- **In-camp:** boil 1 cup water; put the mix in a mug, work 2 tbsp of the hot water into
  a smooth paste, then add the rest and whisk ~20 seconds.
- **Calories:** 199 per serving (stated); protein not stated.
- **Fuel/cleanup:** rides along on any breakfast or dinner boil; mug-only cleanup.
  **[Our note]** skip the cayenne pinch in the kids' bags.
- **Scaling:** pre-bag one per boy per night; the paste-first step is what stops the
  clumpy-cocoa complaints.
- **Rating: top shelf** — the highest morale-per-gram item in the food bag on a 35°F
  Sawtooth night.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Fuel hierarchy of this book:** bag-rehydrate meals (#17, #22, #24) < boil-and-stand
  couscous/noodles (#6, #10–13, #16, #19, #23, #26) < short-simmer (#1, #5, #7, #9,
  #15) < 8–10 min simmer (#2, #3, #4, #8, #20, #21) < 15–20 min (#14 as written, #25).
  The pot-cozy method (verified at Fresh Off The Grid's one-pot roundup,
  https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/one-pot-backpacking-meals/: boil ~60 seconds, then
  wrap and wait) demotes almost any simmer recipe one tier.
- **Cleanup & bears:** the absorb-all-the-water builds (#1, #7, #17) leave no
  food-scented gray water. Cheese pots (#14, #15) are the exception — hot-water swish,
  scattered well away from camp; all wrappers and the salmon pouch go in the hang.
- **Allergy flags for the group:** peanut (#3, #5, #6), tree nut (#10, #12, #19–24),
  dairy (#7, #11, #14–17, #19–21, #23, #26), fish (#9), gluten (all noodle/couscous
  recipes; pages for #2 and #6 list gluten-free swaps).
- **Two known gaps:** the two Skurka recipes with embedded-spreadsheet quantities
  (#6, #18) need the leader to open the live pages once before shopping; everything else
  in this file is shoppable as printed.
