# Frypan, Fresh, No-Cook & Dessert Recipes — Verified

Researched 2026-08-01 for the Sawtooth trip (Aug 10–13, 4 days/3 nights). Companion to
`diy-meals-research.md` (concepts) — this file is the actual recipe book for: (a)
small-frypan meals, (b) night-one fresh dinners, (c) no-cook lunch assemblies, (d)
desserts & hot drinks. One-pot stove dinners and breakfasts live in a separate file.

**Kit assumptions:** canister stove only (campfires banned). The "frypan" is the Toaks
1650 lid — a ~5.5–6 in titanium disc that is thin and hot-spotted (a BPL forum user
measured a 300°F+ spread across a bare titanium pan). Every frypan recipe below assumes:
oil the pan, keep the flame LOW, and slide the pan around over the burner so zones take
turns over the flame. Food hangs at night; nights 30s–40s °F.

**Source labeling:** [Verified] = page opened and read directly this session (or in the
archived earlier pass, noted). [Label] = manufacturer/nutrition-label figure from an
opened page. "Our build" = common-practice quantities, not from a published recipe.
Calories are stated ONLY where a source or label states them.

**Viability scale:** top shelf (plan the menu around it) · solid (works, minor fuss) ·
leader treat (worth it for 2–3 people, not the whole group) · demo only (fun trick,
not a meal plan).

## A. Small-frypan meals (5.5-in titanium lid)

### 1. Pepperoni & cheese quesadilla — top shelf

**Pitch:** pizza flavors in quesadilla form; the highest payoff-per-gram frypan meal for
teens, and the one published recipe in this file that was written for exactly this style
of cooking.

**Source [Verified]:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/pepperoni-and-cheese-quesadilla/

**Serves 1 · 455 cal (stated) · ~10 min · 141 g** (20.4 g protein / 34.2 g fat stated)

Ingredients (per person):
- 1 medium flour tortilla — for this pan use street-taco size (4.5–5 in) so it lies flat
- ~50 g (a generous handful) grated cheddar
- 8 slices shelf-stable pepperoni
- 1 Tbsp tubed tomato paste + 1 tsp dried oregano + 2 Tbsp water
- 1 tsp olive oil

At home: nothing required; pre-grating the cheese into a zip bag saves a knife in camp.

In camp (paraphrased): stir the tomato paste, oregano, and water together in a mug to
make a quick sauce. Warm the oil in the pan on medium, drop the heat to low, and lay in
the tortilla. Once the underside is toasted, flip it, paint on the sauce, scatter the
cheese, and let it melt. Add the pepperoni, fold the tortilla in half, slide it out, and
cut into wedges.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup for this lid:** the recipe's medium-then-low sequence matches titanium
practice exactly. Keep every scrap of cheese inside the fold — cheese that hits bare
titanium carbonizes on a hot spot. One quesadilla per boy, assembly-line style; ~2–3 min
of low flame each, so a whole patrol's worth costs little fuel. Pan wipes clean if no
cheese escaped.

**Why top shelf:** published, quantified, teen-proof, and dense/flat — the food geometry
titanium likes.

### 2. Tortilla trail pizza — top shelf

**Pitch:** hot pizza on day 2 or 3 of a wilderness trip is the single biggest morale
trick per ounce of effort. The published recipe assumes a 6-in pan — exactly this lid.

**Source [Verified]:**
https://www.cloudlineapparel.com/blogs/cloudline/110259334-backpacking-recipe-tortilla-trail-pizza

**Servings: not specified · calories: not stated**

Ingredients (Cloudline specifies items; where it leaves quantity open, amounts below are
our build — common-practice ratios, not from the published recipe):
- 1 six-inch flour tortilla per pizza (the recipe's stated size for a 6-in pan)
- Pizza sauce — Cloudline suggests Boboli 5-oz pouches; one pouch sauces ~4–5 small
  pizzas (our build). A tomato-paste tube + oregano (recipe 1) also works.
- Mozzarella (or cheddar) — ~1 oz / a small handful per pizza (our build)
- Shelf-stable toppings: pepperoni, olives, jalapeños, bacon bits, red pepper flakes,
  parmesan (all from Cloudline's suggested list; pick the spoil-proof ones for days 2–3)

In camp (paraphrased): build the pizza cold — sauce spread thin on the tortilla, then
cheese, then toppings. Set it in the lightly oiled pan and cook over medium-low until
the cheese has melted and the bottom is golden. Cloudline says medium; on titanium start
lower.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup for this lid:** the failure mode is a scorched bottom before the
cheese melts. Three counters: thin sauce layer, a lid or foil tent over the pan to trap
heat on top, and constant slow circling of the pan over the burner. ~3–5 min each.
Sauce drips burn onto titanium — wipe the pan between pizzas.

**Why top shelf:** published for this exact pan size; infinitely customizable; teens
line up for it.

### 3. Camp grilled cheese — solid

**Pitch:** the comfort-food fallback when someone won't eat the planned dinner, and the
best use for bagel halves late in the trip.

**Source:** our build — common-practice quantities, not from a published recipe. (No
strong dedicated backpacking grilled-cheese source located; Dirty Gourmet's backpacking
index was checked and has none.)

**Serves 1 · calories: not stated**

- 2 slices dense bread (sourdough travels best) or 1 bagel, halved — sized to the pan
- 1.5–2 oz sliced block cheddar (pre-slice at home; block cheese survives all 4 days —
  see shelf-life table in `diy-meals-research.md`)
- ~1 Tbsp butter (squeeze bottle or ghee) or olive oil

In camp: butter the outside faces generously. Low flame from the start — bread scorches
on titanium hot spots faster than tortillas do. Toast, pressing lightly, until golden;
flip; keep the pan moving in circles the whole time. Bagel halves go cut-face down first.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup for this lid:** the least titanium-friendly of the "easy" builds
because bread is an insulator with a burnable face — expect one sacrificial slice while
dialing in the flame. ~4–6 min each. Pan wipes clean.

**Why solid, not top shelf:** works, but demands more attention per unit than the
quesadilla, and bread is bulkier than tortillas for the same calories.

### 4. Pan-fried trout, seasoned dredge (two ways) — top shelf

**Pitch:** the meal the trip is half-built around. Dense fish is the documented titanium
success story (BPL frying thread: fried rainbow trout worked where pancakes failed).
Two published dredges — pick one and pre-mix it at home.

**Sources [Verified]:**
- Seasoned flour dredge: https://www.campingrecipes.com/recipes/pan-fried-trout/
  (serves 6–8 on 3–4 trout, ~35 min)
- Cornmeal dredge: https://www.gameandfishmag.com/editorial/simple-pan-fried-trout-recipe/338081
  (serves 4 on 2 lb trout, ~20 min)

**Calories: not stated by either source.**

Dredge A — seasoned flour (campingrecipes.com, for 3–4 trout):
- ½ cup flour · 1 tsp salt · ½ tsp black pepper · ½ tsp oregano · ¼ tsp cayenne
- Fry fat: the source uses 4 Tbsp olive oil + ½ stick butter for a full batch; for this
  pan carry olive oil and add ~1 Tbsp per fish (our scaling)

Dredge B — cornmeal (Game & Fish, for ~2 lb trout):
- ½ cup cornmeal · salt and pepper (garlic powder optional) · butter for the pan

At home: shake the dry dredge together in a quart zip bag. Pack oil in a small leakproof
bottle. A True Lemon packet stands in for the finishing lemon juice.

In camp (paraphrased, merged from both sources): gut and rinse the fish, then pat it
DRY — dry skin is the difference between crisp and stuck. With whole fish, slash 3–4
shallow diagonal cuts per side so it cooks through evenly. Shake the fish in the dredge
bag until coated, tap off the excess, and lay it in hot (not smoking) oil. Roughly 4–5
minutes per side, turning once; it is done when the flesh flakes and lifts from the bone.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup for this lid:** a 5.5-in pan fits one small brookie or a fillet
folded — fry in shifts and let the fastest fisherman eat last. Medium-low flame, pan
constantly circling; titanium's hot center will scorch the dredge if the pan sits still.
~10 min of flame per fish. Wipe the pan while warm; fishy residue MUST go in the hang,
and the wipe rag with it.

**Why top shelf:** documented titanium winner, two published dredges, and it is the
memory the boys will keep.

### 5. Bacon-cheddar hash browns — solid

**Pitch:** diner breakfast smell in the backcountry; a published, quantified recipe
using shelf-stable ingredients.

**Source [Verified]:**
https://www.fusioncraftiness.com/backpacking-hashbrowns-with-bacon-and-cheese/

**Serves 2 · 700 cal/serving (stated) · 15 min prep + 5 min cook**

- 1 cup dehydrated hash browns (Idahoan-style carton)
- Water to cover, for rehydration
- 1 Tbsp oil or ghee
- ¼ cup shredded cheddar
- ¼ cup pre-cooked bacon pieces (shelf-stable, sold near salad croutons)

At home: repackage hash browns into a zip bag; bag the bacon bits and cheese. (The
author notes hard cheese carries fine unrefrigerated for ~3 days; our block-cheddar
shelf-life notes in `diy-meals-research.md` agree for cold-night trips.)

In camp (paraphrased): cover the dry hash browns with water and wait ~15 minutes, then
squeeze the excess water out — a wet pile steams instead of crisping. Heat the fat, fry
the potatoes until they take color, stirring now and then. Off the flame, fold in the
cheese and bacon and serve.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup for this lid:** the recipe was written for a pot; on this lid fry in
two half-batches so the layer stays thin. Titanium will stick some potato no matter
what (BPL thread) — accept a crusty layer, use the full tablespoon of fat, low flame,
keep it moving. ~8–10 min of flame total with rehydration off-flame. Deglaze the pan
with a splash of hot water and drink or scatter it well away from camp.

**Why solid:** quantified and calorie-dense, but the fiddliest routine frypan item on
titanium.

### 6. Silver-dollar pancakes (Kodiak) — demo only

**Pitch:** a two-minute magic trick for morale, NOT a breakfast plan. Batter on bare
titanium is the documented failure case (BPL thread: sticking returned after 1–2 uses
even on seasoned pans; hot spots burn centers while edges stay raw).

**Source [Verified]:**
https://www.trailgroove.com/blogs/entry/337-how-to-make-backpacking-and-camping-pancakes/
**Calories: not stated.**

- Kodiak Cakes (or any just-add-water) mix — TrailGroove carries 1 cup of mix to serve 2
- Water — the article's ratio: 1 cup mix : 1 cup water
- ~2 Tbsp butter + 1–2 oz syrup (TrailGroove's packing amounts)
- For the demo: mini chocolate chips or a few berries

At home: pre-measure 1 cup mix into a quart zip bag (the bag doubles as the mixing bowl
— snip a corner to pipe batter).

In camp (paraphrased): add water to the bag and knead. Melt a thin pat of butter on LOW.
Pour silver-dollar rounds — 2–3 in, one or two at a time, nothing bigger. TrailGroove's
key stove move, which matches the BPL titanium advice exactly: keep the pan moving in an
expanding-and-contracting circle over the flame the whole time so no zone cooks the
center. Flip when bubbles show and the edges set; 1–2 min more on side two.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup for this lid:** re-butter between every pour. Expect the second or
third pancake to start grabbing — that is the titanium evidence playing out, not a
technique failure. Feeding 6 teens this way would burn half a morning and a lot of fuel;
budget it as a 3–4 pancake novelty for whoever is in camp. Soak-and-scrape cleanup.

**Why demo only:** the one published technique that makes it survivable is still fighting
physics; the documented failure case on this exact metal.

## B. Night-one fresh dinners (~6 mi to camp; nights 30s–40s °F)

Food-safety frame for all four: USDA danger zone is 40–140°F, max ~2 hours (1 hour above
90°F) for perishables. The pattern that beats it [Verified —
https://packlitelife.com/how-to/keep-food-cold-backpacking/]: freeze perishables solid at
home so they act as their own ice block thawing through day 1; pack them dead-center in
the pack, insulated by clothing, away from the sun side; eat perishables first. Everything
scented goes in the bear hang, including wrappers.

### 7. Night-one fresh-veg quesadillas — top shelf

**Pitch:** the group dinner. Real vegetables and fresh cheese on the one night weight
doesn't matter, on the technique already proven by recipe #1.

**Source:** our build — common-practice ratios, not from a published recipe. Technique
per trail.recipes quesadilla [Verified, #1]; produce shelf-life per Greenbelly's produce
guide [Verified in the archived earlier pass — bell peppers 3–5 days is community
practice, day-1 use is well inside any bound]. **Calories: not stated.**

Per teen (feeds 6 with one grocery run):
- 2 street-taco flour tortillas (12 total + spares from the lunch supply)
- 2 oz grated cheese (grate a 12-oz block at home; freeze the bag)
- ⅓ bell pepper + 2 Tbsp diced onion (2 peppers + 1 onion for the group, diced at home
  into one zip bag)
- 2 Tbsp canned black beans, drained well, or a few slices of pre-cooked sausage
  (optional protein; one 15-oz can drained ≈ enough for 6)
- Oil for the pan; hot sauce packets

At home: dice all vegetables, bag by ingredient, refrigerate until the trailhead, then
pack frozen cheese against them in the pack core.

In camp: exactly the recipe-1 sequence — oil, tortilla in on low, flip, load half the
face with cheese-veg-beans, fold, press, toast both sides until the cheese binds it.
Two pans of production if another leader's pan exists; otherwise assembly line, ~3 min
per quesadilla.

**Food safety:** cheese and cut vegetables are the perishables; both comfortably survive
one warm day using the freeze-and-center method, and anything uneaten is finished at
this meal — nothing fresh carries to day 2 except whole peppers.

**Why top shelf:** scales to the whole group, one pan, no raw meat risk, and it is
vegetables teens actually eat.

### 8. Night-one brats & tortillas — solid

**Pitch:** maximum teen enthusiasm per minute of cooking. Browned brats, tortilla as
bun.

**Source:** our build — common-practice quantities, not from a published recipe.
**Calories: not stated (check the package label when buying).**

Per teen: 2 fully-cooked bratwursts or smoked sausages (buy PRE-COOKED — e.g. any
"fully cooked" labeled brat/kielbasa — so camp cooking is reheating, not raw-pork
cookery) · 2 tortillas · mustard packets · leftover diced onion/pepper from #7 if both
night-one dinners merge into one feast.

At home: freeze the sealed packages solid; pack core-of-pack per the PackLite method.

In camp: slice each brat lengthwise so it lies flat on the small pan (a whole cylinder
barely touches a 5.5-in disc). Film of oil, medium-low, cut-face down until browned,
roll to finish — ~4 min each, two halves at a time. Serve rolled in a tortilla.

**Food safety:** pre-cooked sausage merely needs reheating, which removes the
undercooked-pork failure mode entirely; frozen-solid packages are still cold at hour 8
of a 6-mile day. Opened leftovers do not keep — cook only what gets eaten.

**Why solid:** trivially easy and loved, but a 5.5-in pan reheats 12 brat halves in
shifts, so the cook eats last; rated below the quesadillas only for throughput.

### 9. Home-cooked foil burritos — solid

**Pitch:** cook dinner in the home kitchen on Sunday, eat it in the wilderness on
Monday. Zero camp prep beyond warming; the fallback if weather slams the first evening.

**Source:** our build — common-practice pattern; the freeze-and-thaw logic is the
PackLite Life method [Verified]. **Calories: not stated.**

Per teen: 1 large burrito — 10-in tortilla + ~½ cup seasoned ground beef or shredded
chicken (cooked fully at home) + ⅓ cup cooked rice + ¼ cup beans + 2 Tbsp cheese.
Roll tight, wrap each in foil, then freeze the wrapped burritos solid.

At home: cook, cool COMPLETELY in the fridge before rolling (rolling warm food and
packing it is the classic danger-zone mistake), wrap, freeze at least overnight.

In camp: burritos have spent the day thawing in the pack core and arrive cool. Warm
each foil-wrapped burrito in the dry pan on low, rolling it a quarter-turn every minute
or two, ~6–8 min — the foil spreads titanium's hot spot for you. Or unwrap and toast
seam-down directly for a crisped skin.

**Food safety:** fully cooked + frozen solid + eaten at hour ~8 keeps the whole timeline
under the danger-zone limit with margin. Night-one only; never carry these to day 2.
Pack out the foil (smellable — it hangs overnight if any survive).

**Why solid:** bombproof logistics and the only dinner that works if the stove itself
fails (they're safe to eat cool); costs a Sunday afternoon at home.

### 10. First-night steak — leader treat

**Pitch:** the PackLite Life page's own conclusion for this method: do the freeze-and-
center steps and "you can enjoy a steak the first night and never miss the cooler."
A leaders' skillet moment after the boys' quesadillas — or a shared bite-for-everyone
flex.

**Source [Verified]:** https://packlitelife.com/how-to/keep-food-cold-backpacking/
(method); quantities are our build. **Calories: not stated.**

- 1–2 sirloin or flat-iron steaks, cut to hand-size so each fits the 5.5-in pan
  (~8–10 oz each; one steak sliced thin feeds 3–4 as tacos on leftover tortillas)
- Salt + pepper (pre-mixed film canister) · 1 Tbsp high-smoke-point oil

At home: season, vacuum-bag or double-zip, freeze rock solid; pack per the method.

In camp: the steak arrives thawed-but-cold. Pat it DRY. Get the oiled pan properly hot
on medium (dense meat is the documented titanium success case — BPL thread), lay the
steak in, and resist moving it for ~2–3 min to build crust; flip and repeat to desired
doneness. Rest 5 min on a clean tortilla (nothing hot rests on bare dirt), slice thin
across the grain, serve as tacos.

**Pan/fuel/cleanup:** highest single-flame item of the trip (~8 min hot burn). Fond
wipes out with hot water; the greasy wipe goes in the hang.

**Why leader treat:** flawless on this kit but only pan-sized portions — feeding six
teens steak from a 5.5-in disc is a shift-work fantasy. As a shared-slices moment it's
unbeatable.

### 11. PB + honey tortilla roll — top shelf

**Pitch:** the standard thru-hiker lunch: fastest calories per minute of effort, zero
cleanup, survives day 4 heat.

**Source:** assembly is common practice; every number is label math (above).

Per wrap: 1 burrito-size tortilla (210) + 2 Tbsp peanut butter (~180–190) + 1 Tbsp honey
(~64 — 86 cal/oz × 0.74 oz) ≈ **455–465 cal**. Two wraps + a handful of raisins is a
~1,000-calorie lunch.

Build: PB to the edges, honey zig-zag down the middle (honey at the edge = glued
fingers), roll tight, eat from the wrapper. At home, repackage PB into a wide-mouth
squeeze tube or buy single-serve cups — a communal jar of PB and six teen spoons is a
hygiene incident scheduled in advance. Allergy check first; sunflower-seed butter swaps
1:1 (calories per its own label).

**Why top shelf:** highest calorie-per-effort item on the menu; nothing to spoil, hang
bag friendly.

### 12. Tuna–cheese–sausage wrap — top shelf

**Pitch:** the savory counterpart — real protein at lunch so dinner portions stop being
an emergency.

**Source:** assembly is common practice; label math as above. Summer sausage calories
vary by brand — read the chub label; not counted below.

Per wrap: 1 burrito tortilla (210) + 1 pouch chunk light tuna 2.6 oz (70 cal, 17 g
protein) + 1.5 oz block cheddar, sliced (~173 — 115 cal/oz) + 3–4 slices summer sausage
(label) + mayo/mustard/relish packets ≈ **~455 cal + sausage** and ~30 g protein.

Build: cheese and sausage sliced onto the tortilla, tuna straight from the pouch (no
drain — buy pouches, not cans), condiment packets, roll. Foil chicken swaps for tuna
1:1 (its own label). Shelf-life logistics: whole cheese block + small sausage chubs
sized to be finished within ~a day of opening — see `diy-meals-research.md` §3.
**The empty tuna pouch is the smelliest trash of the trip — double-bag it and hang it.**

**Why top shelf:** shelf-stable all 4 days, real protein, no stove at noon (which keeps
the fuel math clean).

### 13. GORP trail mix, 1:1 ratio — top shelf

**Pitch:** the graze layer between meals; teens burn out on any single flavor, so the
published 1:1 formula makes variety mechanical.

**Source [Verified]:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/gorp-trail-mix/ —
**302 cal per ½-cup serving (stated; third-party calculator estimate), 12 servings per
batch.**

Published batch (1 cup of each — the stated 1:1 ratio): roasted peanuts, Spanish
peanuts, cashews, raisins, golden raisins, dried cranberries. 6 cups total.

Trip scaling (our math on the stated serving): 2 servings/boy/day × 6 boys × 4 days =
48 half-cup servings = 24 cups = four batches; sub 1 cup of M&Ms or dark chocolate
into each batch 1:1 for morale (chocolate at 169 cal/oz per Greenbelly). Issue each boy
a personal quart bag per day — a communal bag dies by day 2.

**Why top shelf:** published ratio and calories, no prep, and the 1:1 rule lets each boy
build his own batch at the pre-trip meeting (an activity AND the food).

## D. Desserts & hot drinks

### 14. Instant pudding, trail style — top shelf

**Pitch:** cold-water chocolate pudding 5 minutes after dinner, no flame at all — the
easiest "real dessert" in backpacking.

**Source [Verified]:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/double-chocolate-protein-pudding/ —
**354 cal/serving (stated), serves 1, 5 min, no-cook.**

Published single build: ¼ packet instant chocolate pudding + 2 Tbsp milk powder,
bagged together at home; a chocolate-chip protein cookie packed with the snacks. In
camp: powder into a mug, add ½ cup COLD water (creek-cold works best), stir hard 1–2
minutes, wait 4–5 minutes to set, crumble the cookie on top.

Group scale-up (our math, scaling the published ratio ×4): one full 3.4-oz instant
pudding box + ½ cup milk powder + 2 cups cold water in a screw-lid container or
freezer bag; shake 2 minutes, rest 5, spoon into 4 mugs. Two boxes covers 6–8 teens.
Any cookie crumbles on top. (Corroborating community versions of this exact
pudding+milk-powder pattern exist but sit behind fetch-blocked pages — food.com's
"Instant Backpacking Pudding" returned 403 — so the trail.recipes ratio is the verified
anchor.) Instant pudding only — cook-and-serve requires a boil and real milk.

**Fuel/cleanup:** zero flame; the mug licks itself clean via teenager.

**Why top shelf:** no-cook, quantified, cheap, and it converts powdered milk — the
trip's least-loved ingredient — into its most-loved course.

### 15. Easy trail tiramisu (modified for this group) — leader treat

**Pitch:** an absurd flex dessert for the last night: layered "tiramisu" built in a mug
from custard powder and ladyfingers.

**Source [Verified]:** https://trail.recipes/recipes/easy-trail-tiramisu/ —
**492 cal/serving (stated), serves 1, ~20 min.**

Published build (per serving): 1 Tbsp vanilla custard powder + 3 Tbsp full-cream milk
powder (Nido) + 1 Tbsp sugar, bagged at home; 6 ladyfinger biscuits; 1 tsp cocoa; the
published version also uses ½ tsp instant coffee and 1 Tbsp amaretto.

**Group modification (ours, required):** for a church youth group, drop the amaretto
entirely and swap the coffee drizzle for a spoonful of the hot cocoa mix (#16) dissolved
in ¼ cup hot water. Structure is unchanged.

In camp (paraphrased): boil ¼ cup water and dissolve the cocoa (drizzle layer); set
aside. Whisk the custard bag into ¾ cup cold water in the pot, bring to a boil on
medium stirring the whole time, simmer ~1 min until thick, then cool completely (cold
creek dip speeds this). Layer twice in a mug: crushed ladyfingers, cocoa drizzle,
custard. Dust with cocoa.

**Fuel/cleanup:** one short boil + 1 min simmer; the custard pot needs a hot-water
swish. The cooling step is the real cost — start it before dishes.

**Why leader treat:** 20 minutes and pot-cleanup for one serving at a time; perfect for
leaders and the two kids who helped hang the bear bags, silly for feeding eight.

### 16. Hot drinks bar: DIY cocoa + spiced cider — top shelf

**Pitch:** 30s–40s°F nights are the cocoa use case. One home-mixed bag plus a fistful of
cider packets covers every cold evening and the summit break.

**Cocoa — Source [Verified]:** https://www.freshoffthegrid.com/hot-cocoa-mix/ —
**63 cal/cup (stated; third-party calculator estimate), makes ~8 cups.**

Batch: 1 cup dry milk + ½ cup unsweetened cocoa + 6 Tbsp sugar + 2 tsp cornstarch +
½ tsp fine salt — shake together in a bag at home. Serve: ¼ cup mix + 1 cup hot water,
stir. Trip math (ours): one batch per 2 boys for 3 nights; carry it in a doubled
freezer bag with a ¼-cup scoop. Marshmallows ride in their own bag.

**Cider — Source [Label]:** Alpine Spiced Apple Cider single-serve packets (0.74 oz).
Label directions per retail product listings (Amazon/Walmart, opened via search — the
manufacturer page itself was not fetched, so treat as label-via-retailer): empty pouch
into a cup, add 6–8 fl oz hot water, stir. One packet per boy per night; packets are
crush-proof and zero-cleanup.

**Fuel/cleanup:** hot water only — piggyback on the dinner boil; mugs rinse clean.

**Why top shelf:** the highest morale-per-gram item on cold nights, and the cocoa mix
doubles as tiramisu drizzle (#15) and pudding topper.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Fuel ledger for this file:** categories C and 14 are zero-flame; 16 is shared boil
  water. The flame hogs are trout shifts (#4), steak (#10), and any pancake demo (#6).
  Frypan items run LOW flame by design, which also sips fuel.
- **The lid-pan reality:** everything above obeys the three titanium rules from the BPL
  frying thread (oil, LOW flame, keep the pan moving). Dense and flat wins (trout,
  steak, quesadillas); batter loses (pancakes = demo only); leaked cheese and sauce
  carbonize on hot spots.
- **Bear hang additions this file creates:** fish wipe rag, tuna pouches, foil, grease
  wipes, cocoa/cider bags, sausage and cheese. If it touched food, it hangs.
- **Allergy flags:** PB roll and GORP (peanut/tree nut — sunbutter and a nut-free mix
  are the swaps); quesadillas/mac of cheese items (dairy); all tortilla builds (gluten).
- **Source problems hit during research:** food.com blocks fetchers (403) — its pudding
  recipe is corroboration only; thetrek.co/halfwayanywhere/reddit were avoided per the
  research brief; freshoffthegrid.com has no pan-fried trout recipe (the dredges come
  from campingrecipes.com and gameandfishmag.com, both opened); Jif label shows 180
  cal/2 Tbsp on the opened FatSecret page vs 190 on retail listings — carried as a
  range; Alpine cider directions are from retail listings, not the manufacturer page.
