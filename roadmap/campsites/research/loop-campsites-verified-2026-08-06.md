# Alice–Toxaway Loop — Campsite Research, Lake by Lake

**Verified:** August 6, 2026. Every page cited below was opened and read (WebFetch or curl);
no claim rests on a search-result summary alone. Coordinates are USGS GNIS (primary) unless
marked "approx." Camp-area coordinates marked approx are derived from source descriptions +
topo reading and are NOT survey-grade.

**Regulatory frame (already adversarially verified 2026-07-27, see
`roadmap/gear-gap-audit/research/regulations-verified.md`):** no campfires anywhere on the
in-wilderness portion of the loop (Order 0414-04-161, Pettit + Yellow Belly Lake Creek
drainages); camp 100 ft from trails/lakes/streams (teach 200 ft LNT); group cap 12 persons
including leaders; 8+ needs a group permit from SNRA HQ 208-727-5000; TP packed out; wag
bags "strongly suggested" on this loop per the 2026 SNRA Trails Report.

## New primary-source findings this session

- **Wilderness boundary (USFS EDW GIS layer, point queries 2026-08-06):** Yellow Belly Lake
  (44.00111, -114.87567) returns NO wilderness polygon; McDonald Lake's GNIS point
  (43.99847, -114.88950) returns NONE, but points at its west end (-114.8965) return
  "Sawtooth Wilderness." The boundary crosses McDonald Lake. Farley, Toxaway, Twin, Alice,
  Edith all return "Sawtooth Wilderness."
  Query: `https://apps.fs.usda.gov/arcx/rest/services/EDW/EDW_Wilderness_01/MapServer/0/query`
  Corroboration: Leidecker/Outdoor Project Farley page — "mountain bikes can be ridden about
  2 miles up the canyon to the wilderness boundary."
- **Consequence:** the wilderness campfire order does not itself reach a Yellow Belly Lake
  camp (outside wilderness). No SNRA stage restriction as of 2026-08-06 (alerts index:
  Stage 1 Minidoka only; fire danger banner now **Very High / Very High** both zones —
  escalated from July). Recommendation to print: stoves only, every night, everywhere.
- **Order 0414-04-162 does NOT cover the Yellow Belly drainage.** Its Pettit Lake Complex
  (Exhibit E) is the Pettit Lake Creek drainage only. Dispersed camping near Yellow Belly
  Lake is ordinary NF dispersed camping. (Read full order text again 2026-08-06.)
- **2026 SNRA Trails Report (fs.usda.gov/media/247778, re-pulled 2026-08-06):** whole loop
  maintained early July 2026; no Edith campsite-closure note this year (2024 edition had
  "Most campsites at Edith lake closed for vegetation restoration" — still confirm on the
  permit call).

## Lake-by-lake (sources with dates)

**Yellow Belly Lake** — outside wilderness; no developed FS campground ("Thick forest grows
right to a shoreline void of Forest Service campgrounds" — Leidecker/Outdoor Project,
archived); dispersed sites along the very rough 4WD road on the west shore and near the
trail; "the long peninsula and cove on the northeast corner of the lake is a great area";
"great swimming spot on hot days" (sawtoothtrails.com); low congestion. Tent counts:
unverified — no source counts sites. The Dyrt page for the lake is thin/auto-generated and
contradicts Leidecker (claims an "established USFS campground"); do not use it.

**McDonald Lake** — no trip report or guidebook found that documents established campsites;
straddles the wilderness boundary; unbridged Yellowbelly Creek crossing nearby with "a large
well worn log" (idahocampgroundreview.com, Aug 2018). Camping status: unverified.

**Farley Lake** — "The shoreline surrounding Farley Lake is limited in campsites"; cons:
"Popular region. Limited campsites. Mosquitos" (Leidecker). Aug 2018: site found easily
"about halfway up Farley Lake" (~7,800 ft) despite "several parties camped around the lake."
July 2019 (Cheryl Hill): got "a good spot with no neighbors" but was "nervous" arriving
Friday night. Wine Hiking Society: arrived to find it "already pretty full," settled for "a
hilltop spot that was VERY windy and a bit of a trek down to the water." Verdict for 4–6
tents: poor.

**Bowknot / unnamed lake between Farley and Toxaway** — conflicting reports: Evans Outdoor
Adventures (Sept 2015) liked Bowknot ("waterslide," 3 campsites); Cheryl Hill (2019) says
Bowknot "has no campsites" but notes "Lake 8165" with a waterfall; Wine Hiking Society
liked "a spot right on an un-named lake just past Bowknot." Net: 2–3 small sites exist in
this stretch, identity of the lake varies by author. Overflow for 1–2 tents, not a group.

**Toxaway Lake** — best group lake. "Pros: Beautiful shoreline. Great camping." (Leidecker).
"Small footpaths will start appearing on the right. These lead down to nice campsites along
Toxaway that aren't visible from the main trail... you'll have to descend to see what camp
sites are available or are large enough to fit your group" (Wine Hiking Society). A "big
crew" camped on "a grassy spot with a short walk to the beach... plenty of spots on rocks
near the water, and tucked away in trees" (nacforadventure, Labor Day weekend 2020). Outlet-
end rock site at 8,343 ft (Evans, Sept 2015). "Toxaway Lake is huge and has many campsites
surrounding the lake" (Skye Stoury) vs "There aren't a ton of obvious campsites... but you
can certainly find some" (She Dreams of Alpine, Apr 2026) — reconciled by the not-visible-
from-trail point. Wind: three independent reports of afternoon wind. Bugs: worse than Alice
(explorewithalec; Cheryl Hill). Swimming: sandy beach, clear water (voyageswithval;
nacforadventure). Some trailside sites lack water access (Cheryl Hill).

**Toxaway as day-hike base (the layover answer):** "Campers based at Toxaway Lake can day
hike to Sand Mountain Pass, around to Edith Lake, and down to the Toxaway trail for a
6.1-mile loop with 1,468 feet of climbing. It is also a great day hike for backpackers
using Farley Lake as a basecamp." (Leidecker/Outdoor Project Edith Lake page, from
*Exploring the Sawtooths*.) Both segments maintained July 4–5, 2026 per Trails Report.

**Edith Lake** — NOT a group camp: "only two campsites available here... I wouldn't
personally recommend attempting to camp here" (Girl on a Hike, Sept 2020); "Camping isn't
allowed at Edith Lake" (Cheryl Hill, July 2019 — predates the 2024 restoration-closure
note); no equine stock allowed (Leidecker). Confirm current status on the permit call.

**Twin Lakes** — campsites "primarily on the eastern side of the lake (the forested side)"
(Two Outliers); "several flat, scenic campsites and easy access to water... perfect
first-night stop" (Girl on a Hike, late Sept 2020); "an incredible campsite right in between
the two lakes" (Backcountry Post, Aug 2022); quieter than Alice but not empty ("I found a
lot of people do indeed camp there" — Wasatch Wild Child, summer 2021). ~1 mile past and
~400 ft above Alice (Leidecker).

**Alice Lake** — most sites and most people. "There are a lot of campsites around Alice. A
lot." (Wasatch Wild Child, 2021 — also reports trash and bold rodents). Documented
big-group site: "a little peninsula that had plenty of areas for all our tents, as well as
views of water and Sawtooth peaks on 3 sides" (nacforadventure, 2020); north-side peninsula
sites also in Fantasium Media (Sept) and Evans (Sept 2015, "tip of large peninsula,"
8,596 ft). Ranger observed "picking up trash and taking apart fire rings" (nomanbefore,
late Sept). Crowding: "group after group of hikers coming into the lake" (Backcountry Post,
early Aug 2022). Swimming: jump rock, "frigid but refreshing."

**Unnamed lakes below Alice (ponds on Trail 095 ~0.5–1 mi below the outlet)** — no source
documents established sites; one Aug 2022 forum report camped "at the lower lakes"
(ambiguous). Unverified; do not print as a planned camp.

**No privies at any lake on the loop** — no source mentions any toilet; USFS says "wag bags
are strongly suggested" for the loop and Wine Hiking Society reports "We never really found
any good spots... when nature called, so we used our wag bags." (Stated as absence of
evidence + corroborating guidance, not a signed-order fact.)
