The Climb, Drawn Out

Every tenth of a mile, elevation from the USGS 3D Elevation Program. Run your finger along it — the map pin follows. Day 1 does two-thirds of all the climbing; after Snowyside Pass at mile 8.1, the trip is essentially downhill for ten miles.

Main loop — 18.6 mi, ~2,900 ft of climbing

Trailhead 7,014 ft → Snowyside Pass 9,415 ft → out at 7,024 ft · hover / touch to explore (the tooltip shows the grade) · the strip under the chart is the steepness ribbon — same colors as the map's Grade mode

Side trip · Edith Lake

1.0 mi one-way · +490 ft · from Toxaway camp

Leaves the north shore of Toxaway right at camp — the perfect evening or dawn hike. Edith sits in a hanging basin at 8,671 ft, up "beautifully sculpted granite" (Leidecker). Day-hike only: ~2 campsites, and the Forest Service closed most of them for restoration in 2024. Details ↓

Side trip · Sand Mountain Pass

2.0 mi one-way · +890 ft · from the mile-10.9 junction

The climb we cut from the packing route, kept as a pack-free hike: 20 mellow switchbacks to the 9,282-ft divide — Leidecker says it "never really feels like you are gaining much altitude" — with Toxaway below and views across Sawtooth Valley to the White Clouds. Details ↓

No. 03

Three Days, Mile by Mile

Distances measured on the mapped trail lines; climbs from USGS elevation data. Times assume a loaded youth-group pace of ~1.5 mph plus breaks — generous on purpose.

MAug 10

Day 1 — Trailhead → Alice Lake (or Twin) map ↑

6.8 mi+1,925 ft7,014 → 8,883 ft~5–6 hrs

On trail by ~2 PM after the drive. The loop forks a quarter-mile in — stay left along Pettit Lake (the right-hand cutoff is how we come home Thursday). Then steady shade-and-granite climbing up Pettit Creek with lots of loose rock and six creek crossings — in August mostly rock-hops and logs, with the famous no-railing bridge at crossing five (~mile 5). Alice Lake arrives at mile 6.0 (~8,600 ft) under El Capitan; Twin Lakes is 0.9 mi and +260 ft farther and is usually the quieter camp. Water is everywhere on this leg — filter and go, don't carry heavy.

Camp: Twin Lakes preferred · Alice peninsula as fallback · dinner on stoves — no fires, all trip

TAug 11

Day 2 — Over Snowyside Pass → Toxaway Lake map ↑

5.5 mi+534 fthigh pt 9,415 ft @ mi 8.1~4–5 hrs

Leave camp by 7–8 AM — the National Weather Service's mountain rule is to be off high ground by noon. The climb from Twin Lakes to Snowyside Pass is short but rocky, with the last mile fully above treeline; the top is the high point of the entire trip. Then a two-mile switchback descent to Toxaway Lake. The mile-10.9 junction is a three-way where hikers genuinely take the wrong fork: our route turns right (east) along the lake; the fork climbing uphill is the Sand Mountain Pass trail we are not taking with packs. Camp along Toxaway's north shore near the mile-12.3 junction. Arrive by early afternoon: swim (shoreline only — this lake earns a hypothermia warning), fish for brook trout, nap, or take a side trip.

Camp: Toxaway north shore — the best group lake on the loop · afternoon wind is normal, stake tents well

WAug 12

Day 3 — Down the Yellow Belly valley map ↑

4.1–6.3 mi+406 ft8,191 → 7,024 ft~3–5 hrs

The new plan: instead of hauling packs over the old high route, we simply follow the Toxaway drainage downhill — steeply at first through a rocky boulder field beside a chain of waterfalls (a large one has a spur trail to its base), past Farley Lake (mile 14.4, the lunch stop), through open valley to the McDonald Lake junction at mile 16.4. Two endings, Alex's call on the day: (a) camp low near Yellow Belly Lake — it's outside the wilderness boundary, has a swim-worthy northeast cove, and makes Thursday a 2-mile stroll; or (b) push all the way out (6.3 mi total) and car-camp or start driving. Anyone with energy can bank the Edith Lake spur at dawn before packing up.

Camp: Yellow Belly Lake area (option a) · Farley is NOT the group camp — sites are few and small

ThAug 13

Day 4 — Out the cutoff, drive home

~2.1 mi+400 ft over the saddle~1.5 hrs

From the McDonald junction the Pettit Lake Cutoff hops one small forested saddle (7,524 ft) and drops straight back to the cars. Out by mid-morning, lunch in Ketchum or Twin Falls, parents get the ETA text from coverage near Twin Falls.

Drive ~6 hr · no night driving — that's why the fourth day exists

No. 04

Side Trips From Camp

This is where the trip gets great. All three leave from (or next to) the Toxaway camp, all are pack-free, and none are required. Pick by group energy.

The guidebook day loop — Sand Mountain Pass + Edith Lake

6.1 mi loop · +1,468 ft · half a day. Matt Leidecker's Exploring the Sawtooths describes exactly this: "Campers based at Toxaway Lake can day hike to Sand Mountain Pass, around to Edith Lake, and down to the Toxaway trail for a 6.1-mile loop with 1,468 feet of climbing." Both segments were maintained by trail crews July 4–5, 2026. Do it clockwise (pass first) so the climbing happens in cool morning air. The strongest Day-2-afternoon or Day-3-morning option for the fast half of the group.

Just Edith Lake

1.0 mi one-way · +490 ft. Leaves the north shore of Toxaway steps from camp and climbs granite benches into the Edith basin at 8,671 ft. Best evening walk on the trip. Not a camp: reports describe only ~2 sites, one report says camping isn't allowed, and the USFS 2024 trail report noted "most campsites at Edith Lake closed for vegetation restoration" (the 2026 report no longer carries the note, but nothing rescinds it — it's on the permit-call question list). Stock animals are banned overnight within 200 yards.

Just the pass

2.0 mi one-way · +890 ft from the mile-10.9 junction. The switchbacks we're not carrying packs up. Drop packs at the junction on Day 2 and climb light: 20 switchbacks to the 9,282-ft divide with the South Fork Payette. From the saddle — "views all the way back down to and across Sawtooth Valley to the White Cloud Mountains," with Toxaway directly below. Budget 2–3 hrs round trip and watch the sky: it's all exposure up there, and the NWS rule of thumb is to be off high ground by noon.

Camps, Lake by Lake

From a lake-by-lake read of a decade of dated trip reports (verified Aug 6, 2026 — sources in No. 09). Rule of thumb everywhere: camp 100 ft from water and trail (we teach 200), stoves only, hang everything scented.

LakeGroup verdictWhat the reports say
Twin Lakes
8,860 ft · mi 6.8		 night 1 — first choice Sites on the forested east side plus "an incredible campsite right in between the two lakes." Quieter than Alice but not empty. ~1 mi past and ~260 ft above Alice.
Alice Lake
~8,600 ft · mi 6.0		 night 1 — fallback "A lot" of campsites and a lot of people. A north-side peninsula holds a whole group's tents with peaks on three sides. Bold chipmunks & trash reported — hang food, keep a clean camp.
Toxaway Lake
8,323 ft · mi 12.3		 night 2 — the group lake Best group camping on the loop, but the sites hide: watch for small footpaths dropping right (lakeside) from the main trail — the good camps aren't visible from it. Sandy-beach swimming. Afternoon wind is routine; bugs worse than Alice.
Farley Lake
7,745 ft · mi 14.4		 avoid for groups "Shoreline… limited in campsites," popular, and multiple parties report arriving to find it full — one settled for "a hilltop spot that was VERY windy." Lunch stop, not a camp.
Edith Lake
8,671 ft · side trip		 day-hike only ~2 sites, possibly still closed for restoration; one report says camping isn't allowed. Confirm on the permit call; plan to visit, not sleep.
Yellow Belly Lake
7,076 ft · mi ~16.8		 night 3 — option a Outside the wilderness boundary (verified against the USFS GIS layer). No developed campground — dispersed sites near the trail and the rough 4WD road; "the long peninsula and cove on the northeast corner" is called out as the great spot, with good swimming.
McDonald Lake
7,097 ft · mi 16.4		 unverified No source documents established sites; the wilderness boundary crosses the lake. Treat as scenery, not a plan.
Fishing (Idaho license required at 14+)

Toxaway: brook trout (IDFG-observed) — "they can grow to good sizes here." Farley: brook trout (IDFG-observed 2004). Alice & Twin: brook trout per consistent angler reports (IDFG has no survey records for Alice; Twin reportedly fishes better). Yellow Belly: the best diversity on the route — brook, rainbow, westslope cutthroat, and bull trout in IDFG surveys (bull trout must be released). A caught trout pan-fried on the stove is legal and glorious. IDFG Fishing Planner ↗

No. 09

Sources & Method

How this page was built, so a later reader can re-check it instead of re-researching it.

Method

  • Trail lines: OpenStreetMap trail geometry (Pettit Creek, Alice Lake–Redfish Lake, Yellowbelly, Edith Lake, Pettit Lake Cutoff trails), stitched; mileage is arc length along the mapped line.
  • Elevations: 260 samples from the USGS 3D Elevation Program point service, one per 0.1 mi. Cross-checked against published anchors (trailhead ~7,000 ft, Alice 8,596 ft, Snowyside ≈9,400 ft).
  • Grade coloring: a second, denser USGS pass (374 samples, one per 0.05 mi); grade = rise over run, smoothed across a 0.15-mi window (point-to-point DEM grades are noise — every serious tool smooths). Buckets fitted to this trail's real distribution: ±2% flat, then 2–5 / 5–8 / 8–12 / 12%+ (sustained grades here top out ~12% up, ~16% down). Warm-up/blue-down ramp chosen over red/green for colorblind safety and validated (worst up-vs-down confusion ΔE 14.6 under simulated color-vision deficiency).
  • Lake coordinates: USGS GNIS official names database.
  • Camps, hazards, conditions: primary Forest Service pages and signed orders first, then dated first-person trip reports — every cited page was opened and read, none trusted from a search snippet. Anything that couldn't be confirmed is marked unverified.
  • Working files: roadmap/trail-atlas/research/ and roadmap/campsites/research/ in the site repository.
