# Headlamp Category — Adversarial Market Audit (handoff)

**Agent:** MARKET ADVERSARY / headlamp_adversary_1
**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Scope:** "Lighting & Headlamps" category vs. the live 2026 US headlamp market
**Buyer context:** LDS ward scout troop (adults + youth 12–17), Sawtooth Wilderness, Aug 10–13, 2026. 3 nights, no wall power, afternoon-thunderstorm country, price-sensitive youth.

**Bottom line: 2 of 5 picks survive (both need spec corrections). 3 of 5 must be replaced.**
The category was built from stale data: two picks describe superseded versions of themselves, one pick is being retired by its own manufacturer, and one pick's price/water-rating/weight claims are all contradicted by the manufacturer's own product page.

---

## (a) Verdict per current pick

### 1. "Best Overall" — Nitecore UT27 800 ($49 / MSRP $60) → **SURVIVES — FIX SPECS**

The slot survives. The product data doesn't.

- **The current model is the UT27 MCT** (multi-color-temperature UHE LEDs: 3000K CRI-97 warm / 4500K / 6500K + red), sold at **$54.95** on Nitecore's own store. The pre-MCT "UT27 800L" the guide describes is the outgoing version (still ~$49 on Amazon while stock lasts). Guide should name and price the MCT.
  - Source: https://nitecorestore.com/products/ut27-mct-multi-color-temperature-running-headlamp
  - Source: https://www.nitecore.com/product/ut27
- **Battery claim is wrong:** current UT27 MCT ships with the **HLB1500 (1,500 mAh)** Li-ion pack, not the HLB1300. USB-C on the pack, 3×AAA fallback confirmed. (nitecore.com, nitecorestore.com, above)
- Confirmed accurate: 800 lm ✓, 74 g / 2.61 oz ✓, IP66 ✓, 168 m beam ✓, AAA backup ✓.
- **Honesty flag the guide omits:** the UT27's *lowest* white mode is 100 lm / **14.5 h max** — there is no moonlight/reserve mode. Its multi-day story depends entirely on the AAA fallback or a power bank. Fine for a 3-night trip, but the guide should say so instead of implying week-long endurance.
- Independent support for the slot: Backpacker's 2026 guide calls the **UT27 MCT 800** its all-around favorite — so the "#1 for 2026" framing has a real citation behind it, not just backpackers.com.
  - Source: https://www.backpacker.com/gear/essentials/best-headlamps/
- Strongest challenger: **BioLite Range 400** ($59.95, IP67, 200 h low, 8-min fast charge; Treeline Review's 2026 Best Overall, Trail & Kale's "right pick for most hikers"). It loses to the UT27 on brightness (400 vs 800 lm), beam (90 vs 168 m), price, and — decisive for a scout troop — it has **no battery fallback** when a youth forgets to charge. UT27 keeps the slot; Range 400 is the named runner-up.
  - Source: https://www.treelinereview.com/gearreviews/best-headlamps
  - Source: https://www.bioliteenergy.com/products/range-400

### 2. "Premium" — Petzl Actik Core 600 ($64 / MSRP $80) → **REPLACE** with **Fenix HM55R Renegade ($74.95)**

- **The guide describes a superseded product.** Petzl updated the Actik Core in 2025: **625 lm**, USB-C CORE battery, **MSRP $89.95** (street $88 at CleverHiker/Switchback). The "600 lm / $80 / $64 street" data is the old E065AA generation at closeout.
  - Source: https://www.cleverhiker.com/backpacking/best-hiking-backpacking-camping-headlamps/ (lists Actik Core, 625 lm, $88, updated 2026-04-14)
  - Source: https://sectionhiker.com/petzl-actik-core-2025-headlamp-review/
- Even in current form, the Actik Core is **IPX4** — the weakest water rating of any candidate in this category — and at $88-90 it is now *more expensive* than a strictly better-spec'd lamp:
- **Fenix HM55R Renegade — GearJunkie's 2026 Best Overall headlamp:** 1,200 lm (independently bench-measured at 1,130–1,233 lm by 1Lumen — the claim is honest), **IP68 (2 m submersion)**, 175 m beam, 105 g / 3.7 oz, USB-C, **removable** 1,600 mAh Fenix ARB-L18-1600 battery (spares swappable in the field), **$74.95 street / $100 MSRP, in stock**.
  - Source: https://gearjunkie.com/technology/best-headlamps (updated 2026-05-22)
  - Source: https://www.fenixlighting.com/products/fenix-hm55r-renegade-headlamp
  - Source: https://1lumen.com/review/fenix-hm55r-renegade/
- Counter-evidence, disclosed: Switchback Travel (2026-03-27) still names the Actik Core its Best Overall. If the site owner insists on Petzl, the correct listing is **Actik Core 625, $89.95, USB-C** — not the current entry. Petzl Swift RL 2.0 (1,200 lm reactive, 92 g, ~$135) was considered and rejected on price for this audience.
  - Source: https://www.switchbacktravel.com/best-headlamps
  - Source: https://www.petzl.com/US/en/Sport/Headlamps/SWIFT-RL

### 3. "Budget" — Black Diamond Spot 400-R ($39 / MSRP $50) → **REPLACE** with **Petzl Tikkina ($19.95)**

This is the worst entry in the category. Black Diamond's own product page contradicts four of the guide's claims:

- **MSRP is $79.95, not $50.** Current BD sale price **$63.96, not $39** — and the lamp is **out of stock** at BD.
- **IP67, not IPX8.** BD's page: "waterproof to operate at least 1.0 meter underwater for 30 minutes."
- **86 g = 3.0 oz, not 2.6 oz.**
- **Micro-USB confirmed** — a genuine liability in 2026 when every other lamp in the lineup (and every scout's charger) is USB-C.
  - Source: https://www.blackdiamondequipment.com/en_US/product/spot-400-r/
- The "225-hr" figure is a 6-lumen reserve-mode number. OutdoorGearLab's standardized "light coffin" testing has repeatedly found the BD Spot line's claims the worst in the business — measured low-mode runtime a fraction of claims ("wildly misleading battery claims") and water intrusion despite the rating ("water readily flows inside") on the earlier Spot. Treat all Spot endurance marketing as unverified.
  - Source: https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/topics/camping-and-hiking/best-headlamp
- **Replacement — Petzl Tikkina, $19.95 MSRP:** 300 lm, 92 g, IPX4, 3×AAA *included* (no charging infrastructure needed — leaders just carry spare AAAs), 100 h claimed low (10 lm mode: 10 h at 40 m — real usable numbers), and it is **HYBRID: drop in a Petzl CORE battery ($34.95) later for USB-C rechargeability.** It is the named budget pick of both GearJunkie and CleverHiker for 2026; Switchback's budget pick (BD Astro 300, $24.95, also AAA/IPX4) is the acceptable alternate.
  - Source: https://www.petzl.com/US/en/Sport/Headlamps/TIKKINA
  - Source: https://gearjunkie.com/technology/best-headlamps
  - Source: https://www.switchbacktravel.com/best-headlamps
- Rejected: BD Astro 300-R ($44.95) — micro-USB, IPX4, 100 g, out of stock at BD. Strictly dominated.
  - Source: https://www.blackdiamondequipment.com/en_US/product/astro-300-r/

### 4. "Ultralight" — Nitecore NU25 UL 400 ($29 / MSRP $37) → **SURVIVES — FIX SPECS**

The right lamp, described one generation late.

- **Current model is the NU25 MCT UL** (multi-color-temperature UHE LEDs): **700 mAh** (not 650), **132 m beam** (not 64 m — the UHE emitter doubled it), 47 g / **1.66 oz** (guide's 1.6 oz is close), **$36.95** at Nitecore Store (guide's $29 street matches old-version closeouts).
  - Source: https://nitecorestore.com/products/nu25-multi-color-usbc-lightweight-headlamp
  - Source: https://www.nitecore.com/product/nu25ul
- Confirmed: 400 lm ✓, IP66 ✓, USB-C ✓, 45 h low ✓ (at 6 lm ultralow — say so).
- Slot is independently backed: CleverHiker's Ultralight pick and Switchback's Best Ultralight for 2026. No credible challenger at this weight/price (Petzl Bindi: 35 g but 200 lm, IPX4, micro-era design, ~$60 — dominated).

### 5. "Youth" — BioLite HeadLamp 325 ($32 / MSRP $40) → **REPLACE** with **Nitecore NU21 ($29.95)**

- **The HeadLamp 325 is being retired.** BioLite's own product URL now 404s, and BioLite's gear guide confirms the 325 is leaving the lineup as the brand consolidates on the Range/Dash series. Recommending a discontinued, micro-USB, IPX4 lamp to the most-likely-to-drown-it audience is indefensible.
  - Source (404): https://www.bioliteenergy.com/products/headlamp-325
  - Source: https://www.bioliteenergy.com/blogs/gear-guides/which-biolite-headlamp-is-right-for-you
- **Replacement — Nitecore NU21, $29.95:** 360 lm, 44 g / 1.55 oz, **IP66**, **USB-C** (1.5 h fast charge), 37 h max runtime, simple two-button interface, ~$5 cheaper than the outgoing BioLite. Better water rating, better port, lighter, brighter — at the youth price point.
  - Source: https://www.nitecore.com/product/nu21
  - Source: https://nitecorestore.com/products/nitecore-nu21-ultralight-rechargeable-headlamp
- Alternate if the troop prefers no-charging simplicity: Petzl Tikkina ($19.95, AAA) — but it's already the Budget pick; keeping the slots distinct favors the NU21.

**Marketing-runtime reality check (applies category-wide):** every "200+ hour" headline number in this market (BD 225 h @ 6 lm, BioLite 200 h @ 1.5 lm, Fenix "up to 120 h" on lowest flood) is a moonlight/reserve-mode figure at 1.5–6 lumens — enough to read a map, not to walk trail. The rewritten category copy should state runtimes as "X h at Y lumens," which the recommended lineup data below does. Conversely, the UT27's numbers are honest but short: its floor is 14.5 h because its lowest mode is a genuinely usable 100 lm.
