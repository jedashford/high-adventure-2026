No. 04

Safety & Communication

What parents should know about how the group stays safe out there.

Reaching the group

There is zero cell service on the entire loop — all four days. Plan of record: leaders carry a satellite communicator (SOS + two-way text) for emergencies, and parents get an ETA text Thursday when the group is back in coverage (around Twin Falls, mid-afternoon). confirm details at pack check

No campfires — permanent

There will be no campfire on this trip, any night. The Forest Service names our exact drainages in a year-round closure: campfires are prohibited in the Alice/Twin Lakes and Toxaway/Farley Lakes drainages. Alice → Twin → Toxaway → Edith → Farley — every camp we have is inside that ban. It is not a fire-season rule that might lift; it is permanent, because these basins get so much use the wood is gone. Three more rules stack on top: no ground fires anywhere in the Sawtooth Wilderness (fire pan or blanket only, where fires are legal at all), no fires more than ¼ mile off-trail July 1–Labor Day, and wood-burning backpacking stoves are banned wherever fires are. If you find a rock fire ring out there, it is illegal — don't use it, and don't build one. USFS Sawtooth Wilderness regulations.

Why it matters — Jul 12, 2026

The Cabin Creek Fire started near Alturas Lake — the next basin north of our trailhead — when someone left a campfire unattended after cooking a hot dog in foil. Human-caused, 7 acres, contained. That is how close to home this gets, and it's two weeks before we leave. Cooking happens on the stoves. Nowhere else.

Cooking & fish

All meals run on the three canister stoves — those stay legal even under Stage 1 fire restrictions, because they have a shut-off valve. One catch worth knowing: the stove must sit in a spot cleared of anything flammable for three feet. On granite slab by the lake, easy. On pine duff, scrape a patch first. Catch a trout and you can absolutely eat it — pan-fried on the stove. Anyone 14 or older needs an Idaho fishing license.

Storms

August pattern is blue mornings, thunderstorms after lunch. The plan keeps the group over the passes before ~1 PM. Lightning drill: off ridges, spread out, crouch on packs, wait 30 min after the last thunder.

Cold

Lake basins sit at 8,000–8,700 ft — nights in the 30s–40s even in August. Every boy carries a warm layer, beanie, and dry sleep clothes that never get worn hiking.

Water crossings & snow

July reports: creek crossings plus lingering snow near the Edith Lake pass (mostly melted by Aug 10). Crossings are done with hip belts unbuckled, water shoes on, facing upstream.

Bears & critters

Black bear country — no grizzlies in the Sawtooths, which is exactly why the rules here are lighter than Yellowstone's. A bear canister is not legally required inside the Sawtooth Wilderness: the Forest Service food-storage order specifically excludes the wilderness, so a proper hang is both legal and sufficient. Nobody needs to buy a $100 canister for this trip. The Forest Service's own standard for a hang is “at least 10 feet clear of the ground at all points and four feet horizontally from any supporting tree or pole.” Nothing scented in a sleeping bag — and note that the Forest Service counts personal hygiene products and empty wrappers as food. Toothpaste, deodorant and lip balm hang with the food. The real thieves up there are chipmunks, marmots and gray jays, not bears. The trailhead has a stricter rule that is enforced ↓

Altitude & sun

The trailhead is already at 7,000 ft. Hydration starts on the drive up; sunscreen goes on every 2–3 hours — UV at 9,000 ft burns fast.

Wilderness rules

Free permit · group cap of 12 · catholes 200 ft from water with TP packed out · dogs leashed through Labor Day. All of it — plus the phone number you have to call, the new trailhead fee, and the campsite limits — is in No. 05, Permits, Fees & Limits.

No. 05

Permits, Fees & Limits

Verified against the signed Forest Service orders in July 2026 — not the summary web pages, which contain at least two errors. Four things here need a phone call or a decision before we leave.

Do this first — the permit call

A group of 8 or more cannot self-issue at the trailhead. We have to get a large-group permit in advance, and it's free. Call the Sawtooth NRA Headquarters: 208-727-5000 — 5 North Fork Canyon Road, Ketchum. Hours are narrow: 8:30 AM – 4:30 PM weekdays only, and that desk is only staffed June 15 – Sep 7. Call on a weekday morning. Do not drive to the Stanley Ranger Station — its own page now reads “Hours of Operation: Closed to visitors.” needs doing

The cap is 12 people — leaders included

The binding order prohibits being in the wilderness “with a group of more than 12 persons.” Not 12 boys. Not 12 participants. Twelve human beings — every Young Man, every adult leader, every dad who hikes in for a night. There is no leader exemption anywhere in the order. A group of 13 is a Class B misdemeanor: up to $5,000 for an individual, $10,000 for an organization, and/or up to six months. If the roster goes over, the Forest Service's own standard for splitting is that the two groups be “separated by a drainage or a day apart” — two genuinely separate trips with separate permits and camps, not one group walking in two clumps. On a 22-mile loop that's a real itinerary change. Lock the final headcount before the Jul 29 pack check. decision needed

The trailhead is renamed — and now costs money

Tin Cup Trailhead is officially Pettit Lake Trailhead now, so that's the name on the signs and on the Forest Service page. There is a $10/day fee, and it is cash or check only — no cards, no app. Somebody needs to bring actual cash. An annual pass is $100 if that math works better for four days. Confirm on the permit call whether we're charged per day or a flat overnight rate.

The gate can turn us away

When the trailhead hits 100% occupancy, the hosts “implement a one-in for every one-out policy.” A party of 12 arriving at a full trailhead can be held at the gate. Ask HQ on the permit call how that's applied to permitted groups, and have a backup start time. You'll also be asked to register last name, party size, length of stay, and license plate — so have the plate numbers for every car.

Car-camping the night before? Two new limits

Two forest orders issued weeks ago change this. A single developed campground unit is capped at 8 people (order 0414-04-162, effective June 19, 2026) — so 12 of us need a double unit or two sites. And a unit is limited to 2 vehicles (order 0414-04-163, effective July 1, 2026), which matters for a 3–4 car caravan out of Provo. Dispersed camping between Forest Roads 208/362 and Pettit Lake is prohibited — we have to be in the developed campground. One more wrinkle: being at a developed rec site between 10 PM and 6 AM is restricted, which complicates a pre-dawn start.

Food storage: the parking lot is the risk, not the backcountry

This is the opposite of how everyone assumes it works. Inside the wilderness, no canister is required and a proper hang is fine. But the food-storage order binds the trailhead and Pettit Lake Campground in full, and it's actively enforced there. Everything smellable must be inside a closed hard-sided vehicle with the windows up, or an IGBC-certified container — unless someone awake is within 100 feet and line-of-sight of it. The order defines “food and refuse” to include personal hygiene products and empty food containers, so a packed-out trash bag in an open truck bed is a violation. Same $5,000 / $10,000 penalty.

Ask about Edith Lake

The 2024 trail report said “most campsites at Edith Lake closed for vegetation restoration.” The 2026 report no longer carries that note — but nothing anywhere affirmatively rescinds it. Edith Lake is in our route name and it's a planned camp, so ask directly on the permit call. needs confirming

Toilet paper packs out — that's a rule, not etiquette

The Forest Service is explicit: “Toilet paper should never be buried and needs to be carried out with users.” And because ground fires are banned wilderness-wide, burning it isn't an option either. Every person needs a sealed bag for used TP. Catholes are 6–8 inches deep. The Forest Service page says 100 ft from water; Leave No Trace and Scouting America both say 200 ft. We teach 200.

Trail status — good news

Pettit Lake (Tin Cup) Trailhead is open and every segment of our loop was trail-crewed this season: the loop was maintained Jul 2 and Alice Lake was cleared Jun 18. Grandjean Trailhead is closed for debris flows, but it's on the opposite side of the wilderness and doesn't touch our route. Fire restrictions as of late July are on the Minidoka Ranger District only — not us — but August escalation is routine. Re-check the alerts page within 72 hours of departure. The Trail & Conditions

Rated Hard · 4.8★ from 282 AllTrails reviews · elevation runs 6,755 → 9,709 ft. The loop links Petit Creek, Alice Lake–Redfish Lake, Edith Lake, Yellowbelly Lake, and Petit Lake Cutoff trails — counterclockwise from Pettit Lake.

Know before we go

  • Permit: free — but groups of 8+ must contact the Forest Service in advance; trailhead self-issue only covers smaller groups. Group cap is 12 people (see alert at top).
  • Parking: a recent report says Pettit Lake TH charges $10 — bring cash. Lot fills by 9 AM; we arrive afternoon, so expect overflow parking.
  • Water: lakes and creeks the whole way — group carries filters; each hiker needs 3–4 L of capacity.

Fire & trail alerts (checked Jul 27)

Campfires are permanently banned on our whole route — see Safety. Beyond that: SNRA fire danger is rated Very High. Stage 1 fire restrictions are in effect on the Minidoka district (not ours) as of Jun 30, but the SNRA and Sawtooth Wilderness went Stage 1 on Aug 1 last year — expect it to be active when we're there. It changes nothing for us: canister stoves stay legal, and we already have no fire. Grandjean Campground and Trailhead closed Jul 23 (debris flows) — not our trailhead, Tin Cup is unaffected. Regional haze from eastern Oregon fires. Warm winter, low snowpack, drought forecast to hold through summer. Re-check the Sawtooth alerts page the week we leave.

The Drive

380 miles, about 6 hours door-to-trailhead via I-15 N → I-84 W → US-93 N → ID-75 N (fastest of three route options per Google Maps). Leaving at 6:00 AM puts us at the trailhead around 1:00–1:30 PM with two stops.
