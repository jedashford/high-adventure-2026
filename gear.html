🔥 Stove Comparison — All 11, Side by Side

Do you need a pressure regulator on this trip? No.

A regulator holds the burner’s output steady as canister pressure falls. Pressure falls for two reasons: cold, and the canister running low. Our trip is August 10–13, camps at 8,000–8,700 ft, nights in the 30s — so a morning canister sits around 35–45°F. Isobutane doesn’t start losing pressure until about 11°F. We are 25+ degrees clear of the problem a regulator solves.

The head-to-head test below had to chill canisters to −9°C (16°F) before a difference appeared — and even then both stoves still boiled in about two minutes. At our temperatures, regulated and unregulated behave the same.

What actually decides fuel use is the heat exchanger, not the regulator. The two Integrated Jetboils are unregulated and still get ~23–27 L per canister, while every regulated bare burner sits far below that. Spend on the heat exchanger and the wind shielding; treat a regulator as a nice-to-have you should not pay extra for this August.

The one case it would matter: a shoulder-season or winter trip, or running canisters to truly empty. If you ever want both, the Jetboil MiniMo is the only regulated integrated system here.

Fuel — all three builds: one 230 g canister covers the whole trip for two. Three dinners plus three breakfasts is about 8 L of boiling, and even the least efficient setup here gets ~16 L from a 230 g can. Don’t buy a second one, and don’t pay extra for fuel efficiency at this scale — that only matters for the 12-person group kit.

Note the inversion: unlike tents and bags, spending more on cooking does not buy you less weight here — the Budget kit is the lightest of the three at 6.5 oz, beating the $164.99 Stash. What the extra money buys is a pot that locks to the burner and one thing to pack instead of two. Worth it around a 14-year-old; not worth it for grams.

Titanium or aluminium? For boiling plain water, titanium wins — and that is all this trip does. Titanium conducts heat roughly 1/11th as well as cookware aluminium (~22 vs ~190 W/m·K). That scorches a ring into anything with substance — stew, oatmeal, ramen — even at a nominal boil, because solids sitting against the wall don’t convect away. It is genuinely a non-issue for plain water, which holds the wetted wall near 100°C whatever the metal. We boil water and pour it into pouches, so the drawback never bites here — but it would the moment you cooked in the pot.

Is there a titanium heat-exchanger pot? Not on sale today — but it has been done, so this is a manufacturing problem, not a physics one. Jetboil’s Sol Ti (2011) welded an aluminium FluxRing to a titanium body. Titanium and aluminium expand at different rates, so the fins melted or detached in the field and the joint corroded; Jetboil discontinued it and nobody has repeated the design since. Every heat-exchanger pot sold now is aluminium-bodied, and TOAKS, Evernew, Keith, Vargo and Boundless Voyage make none. Since one 230 g canister covers this trip either way, choosing titanium costs you nothing. The TOAKS 1300 ml is 1.7 oz lighter and 300 ml bigger than the 1 L aluminium heat-exchanger pot, for $23 more.

Why 1.3 L and not 1 L: two freeze-dried pouches need up to 2 cups each, about 950 ml. In a 1 L pot that is brim-full and cannot reach a rolling boil without spilling — so a 1 L pot still means two boils. 1.3 L holds it with real headroom. That headroom, not the fins, is what saves you a second boil at every meal.

Cooking prices and weights verified against each manufacturer 2026-07-29. Tent, bag, pad and pillow figures in this box predate that audit and are still approximate.

🐟 Trout Fry Kit — optional add-on ~$46 · ~10.3 oz

Only if somebody plans to fish. The packing list already says anyone frying a trout brings their own pan, and the whole loop is a permanent no-fire zone — so the fish gets fried on the stove, never over a fire. Idaho licence required at 14 and up.

  • Pan: Fire-Maple Feast 7.6 in non-stick ($29.95 · 9.2 oz) — or the MSR Ceramic Skillet at 6.7 oz for $61.95 if the weight matters more than $32
  • Turner: GSI Compact Scraper ($8.95 · 0.63 oz) — flips the fish and scrapes the pan, and it is the only utensil verified as explicitly non-stick safe
  • Soap: Wilderness Wash Pocket Soap ($6.95 · 0.5 oz) — already on your list
  • Oil: a small bottle. No manufacturer we checked actually claims leakproof — humangear and Nalgene both stay silent on it. Test it at home upside-down before it rides in your food bag

⚠ Titanium is the wrong pan for fish. Trout skin welds to bare metal, and titanium's hot spots make it worse. TOAKS sells titanium pans at 2.5–3.7 oz and they are the wrong tool here — non-stick is the whole point.

⚠ This is the most odorous thing you will do out there, in black bear country. Fry well away from where you sleep. The pan, the scraper, and every scrap go in the hang with the food, 100 ft from tents — grease included. An OPSak helps, but its own long-term reviewer found the outside of the bag picks up food smell from handling within a few days, so treat it as odour reduction, never a substitute for the hang.
