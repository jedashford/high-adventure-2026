No. 02

One-Pot Dinners

Eighteen dinners for the 1.6 L pot, best first. The three full cards are the ones to build the menu around; everything after them is complete and shoppable, just condensed. Couscous is the fuel cheat code — it never sits on the flame — and the pot-cozy trick (No. 08) demotes almost any simmer by a tier.

🍜 The Ramen Bomb top shelf

The thru-hiker classic: the potatoes drink the ramen water, so nothing gets drained and everything gets eaten. It's ramen — it cannot be ruined and nobody has to be talked into it.

cal not stated~5 min flamezero drain water
One very hungry hiker
  • 1–2 packets ramen (one seasoning sachet is enough)
  • ¼–½ pouch instant mashed potatoes
  • 1 foil pack Spam, tuna, or chicken
  • Endorsed add-ins: Cheetos, chips, cheese
Feeds 2–3 (our build)
  • 3–4 ramen packets + 1 full 4-serving Idahoan pouch + 2 protein pouches, 2 sachets
In camp
  1. Boil the ramen in generous water with its seasoning — extra water is fine, you will not drain it.
  2. Noodles done → kill the flame.
  3. Stir in the potato flakes; they absorb the remaining water almost instantly.
  4. Stir in the protein and add-ins.

One of the best fuel-per-calorie meals in this book; the starch absorbs everything, so the pot wipes clean. Source: hike.guide (deliberately loose quantities; group scaling is our build).

🌮 Beans + Rice with Fritos & Cheese (Skurka) top shelf

The most-requested dinner on Andrew Skurka's guided trips — a walking taco in a pot, quantified to the tenth of an ounce, 60 seconds of simmer.

668 cal · 27 g protein (stated)~5 min flame117 cal/oz
Per serving
  • 2 oz instant refried beans
  • 1.5 oz instant rice
  • 1 oz cheddar cheese, diced
  • 1 oz Fritos
  • 0.2 oz (≈2 tsp) taco seasoning
  • 10+ oz water
At home

Bag beans + rice + seasoning together — one 3.5 oz snack bag per person; cheese rides separately. The dry bags keep 6–9 months.

In camp
  1. Boil the water first — solids added early scorch.
  2. Pour in the bag; simmer only ~60 seconds, stirring.
  3. Off heat: stir in the cheese.
  4. Fritos at the last second so they stay crunchy. Serve in tortillas for burritos.

Best fuel-to-calories ratio of any dinner here; everything absorbs, one hot-water swish for the cheese film. Three bagged servings cook together in the Toaks with ~30 oz water. Source: andrewskurka.com.

🍗 Chicken and Rice top shelf

Greenbelly's four-ingredient comfort classic. The plainest, safest dinner in the book — nobody has ever rejected chicken and rice.

cal not stated~10 min flame (or cozy it to ~2)
Per serving (grams as stated)
  • 100 g pre-cooked chicken pouch
  • 80 g instant rice
  • 35 g dried broccoli (specialty buy — the meal is complete without it)
  • 20 g olive oil
  • 3 g soy sauce
In camp
  1. Boil water at 2:1 water-to-dry.
  2. Broccoli in for 5 minutes, then rice for 5 — or boil 2 minutes and cozy-wrap 10.
  3. Off heat, stir in chicken, soy, and the oil.
Scaling

Double the grams for two. The olive oil is the cheapest calorie lever there is (250 cal/oz) — don't skip it.

Absorb-style, no drain. Source: Greenbelly.

The solid shelf — complete and shoppable, condensed

🍜 Jerky Ramen solid

Ramen upgraded into brothy beef-noodle soup — the jerky softens in the hot broth.

388 cal · 17.6 g protein (stated)~8–9 min flame

Per serving: 1 packet dashi powder (5 g) or 1 tsp vegetable bouillon · ¼ cup dehydrated vegetable mix (optional in practice) · 1 block ramen (~50 g) · 25 g beef jerky · 1½ cups water.

Camp: broth + vegetables into the water, boil, simmer low ~5 min; add ramen and jerky, 3–4 min more. Light for a big appetite — run 1.5 blocks per person; brothy pot gets drunk clean.

Dashi is a specialty item; the page itself blesses the bouillon swap. Source: trail.recipes.

🥜 Spicy Peanut Butter Ramen solid

Pad-thai energy from a five-ingredient sauce mixed at home. No draining.

463 cal · 14.9 g protein (stated)~8 min flamepeanut allergy check

Per serving: 1 block ramen (~50 g) · ¼ cup dehydrated vegetables (optional) · sauce: 1 Tbsp peanut butter + 1 Tbsp soy + 1 tsp rice vinegar + 1 tsp sriracha (halve for milder palates) + 1 clove minced garlic · ⅔ cup water.

Home: mix the sauce, decant into a small bottle (triple batch in one 4 oz bottle for a triple-batch pot). Camp: vegetables + water, boil, simmer ~5; ramen in 3–4 min until the low water mostly absorbs; off flame, stir the sauce through.

Source: trail.recipes.

🍋 Chili Lime Peanut Noodles solid

Sweet, limey, crunchy — 716 stated calories out of one ramen packet.

716 cal · 18 g protein (stated)one short boilpeanut allergy check

Per serving: 3 oz ramen (seasoning discarded) · 1 Tbsp brown sugar · ¼ tsp ground ginger · ¼ tsp red pepper flakes · 1 Tbsp chopped peanuts · 1 peanut-butter packet (2 Tbsp) · 1 True Lime packet.

Camp: water to barely cover the noodles, boil until done; pour off all but ~¼ cup (the book's only drain step — arc it well away from camp, or just start with less water); stir in spice bag, PB, and lime until creamy.

Source: Fresh Off The Grid.

🥡 Peanut Noodles (Skurka) solid

The guided-trip crowd favorite — 809 stated calories, boil-and-soak, no simmer at all.

809 cal · 141 cal/oz (stated)boil + 5 min soakpeanut allergy check

Per serving: ramen (flavor packet discarded) + crushed peanuts/cashews/raisins · sauce made entirely at home: peanut butter, soy, garlic, ginger, lime juice, toasted sesame oil, olive oil, honey — carried in a 4–8 oz wide-mouth bottle (the page warns 2 oz jars pour miserably). Meal weight 5.7 oz stated.

Camp: boil 8+ oz water, add ramen and nuts, kill the stove, sit 5 min; stir the sauce through. Gluten-free rice-ramen swap is on the page.

Caveat: exact sauce quantities live in the page's embedded spreadsheet, which resists scraping — open the live page once before shopping. Rated solid until you've pulled the sheet; cooks like a top-shelf meal after.

🐟 Teriyaki Salmon Rice Bowl solid

A five-minute rice bowl — dead simple, but poll the group before buying six salmon pouches.

353 cal · 19.8 g protein (stated)~5 min flame

Per serving: ⅓ cup instant rice · 1 Tbsp nori, cut in strips · 1 Tbsp teriyaki sauce (small bottle) · 1 pouch pink salmon (~2.5 oz) · 1 tsp sesame seeds (optional).

Camp: rice + ⅓ cup water, boil ~5 min stirring; stir in nori, top with salmon, drizzle, sprinkle. 353 cal is light — run 2–3× per person. The salmon pouch is bear-hang priority trash — double-bag it.

Source: trail.recipes.

🍅 Sun-Dried Tomato Couscous solid

Dietitian-written, all-grocery, pizza-adjacent — and the pot never touches the flame.

636 cal · 18 g protein · 127 cal/oz (stated)boil-only

Per serving: ½ cup (86 g) couscous · 3 pieces (15 g) sun-dried tomatoes, diced · 2 Tbsp (20 g) pine nuts (slivered almonds is our cheaper swap) · ½ tsp each dried parsley, oregano, basil, turmeric · ½ tsp garlic powder · ¼ tsp black pepper · ⅛ tsp salt · 1 Tbsp (14 g) olive oil · 6 oz water.

Camp: hot water into the bag or pot, stir, stand a few minutes; stir in the oil. Add a 7 oz chicken pouch per two servings to make it a main course (our note). Rehydrated in the bag = the pot stays clean.

Source: Backcountry Foodie.

🫒 Mediterranean Couscous solid

Four ingredients: couscous, parmesan, pesto mix, olives. Olives split the room — let everyone opt in.

cal not statedboil-only

Per serving (grams as stated): couscous 85 g · parmesan 35 g · dry pesto mix 20 g · olives 20 g (shelf-stable pouches survive the hang fine).

🌶 Chili Mac and Cheese solid

Macaroni + beans + cheddar + chili powder — guaranteed crowd-pleaser flavors; the simmer time is the tax.

cal not stated~15 min as written — cozy it down

Per serving (grams as stated): dried beans 80 g · raw macaroni 80 g · cheddar 80 g (cube at home) · chili powder 10 g.

Camp: boil at 2:1; beans 10 min, macaroni 5 more, off heat stir in cheddar + chili powder. Our fix for the fuel: use instant refried or dehydrated pre-cooked beans (never raw dry beans) to collapse the bean step, and cozy the pasta (boil 2, wrap 10). Cheese pot needs a proper hot-water swish, scattered away from camp.

Source: Greenbelly.

🧀 Good Old Mac N Cheese solid

A real cheese-sauce ratio instead of neon powder — the best-tasting mac formula here, for leaders willing to prep at home.

656 cal · 19.1 g protein (stated)~6–7 min flame

Per serving: sauce bag: 1 Tbsp heavy-cream powder (Nido works, per page) + 3 Tbsp cheddar cheese powder + ½ tsp cornstarch · ⅔ cup pre-cooked and dehydrated pasta · salt · 1 Tbsp ghee.

Camp: pasta + ⅓ cup water, boil then ~5 min stirring; off heat, sauce bag in, then the ghee. The 3:1:½ sauce ratio scales linearly — one bag per person.

Fussiest shopping list in the book: home-dehydrated pasta + cheese powder (online buy). If that's not happening, make the chili mac above. Source: trail.recipes.

🌿 Pesto Noodles (Skurka) solid

Ramen + parmesan + sun-dried tomato + basil + olive oil. No simmer; rarely refused once it smells like pizza.

700 cal · 125 cal/oz (stated)boil-and-soak

Per serving (from the page's own comments — the main table is an embedded sheet): noodles 3.00 oz · garlic powder 0.05 oz · dried basil 0.05 oz · parmesan 0.80 oz · sun-dried tomatoes 0.80 oz · olive oil 1.00 oz · ~10 oz water. Meal weight 5.6 oz stated.

Camp: water boiling or near it, everything in except parmesan (tomatoes first, ramen seasoning discarded), stir, soak — the page is explicit no simmer is needed. Off stove, parmesan through. Group pattern: each person a noodle ration; parmesan/oil/tomato in shared containers. Add-ins the page suggests: salami, jerky, nuts.

Source: andrewskurka.com.

🥔 3-Cheese Mashed Potatoes with Kabanos solid

Rehydrates entirely in its own bag — the stove only ever boils ⅔ cup of water, and there is zero pot cleanup. Also the designated rescue meal: carry one spare bag per group.

411 cal · 22.3 g protein (stated)cheapest fuel in the bookbag = bowl

Per serving: ½ cup instant mashed potatoes · 1 Tbsp each freeze-dried cheddar, parmesan, mozzarella (powdered) · 1 Tbsp crispy fried onions (optional) · 4 shelf-stable Kabanos sausages (~50 g).

Camp: boil ⅔ cup water, pour into the bag, mix, stir in sliced sausage. Grocery-store reproduction (ours, not the source's): a flavored Idahoan pouch + Slim Jim-class snack sticks. 411 cal is a side for a hungry hiker — double the potatoes or add a soup packet.

Freeze-dried cheese powders are an online buy; Kabanos lives in European delis. Source: trail.recipes.

🫘 Backcountry Chili (Skurka) solid

Client-trip chili: refried beans, tomato powder, TVP, cheese, corn chips. Boil, cozy, done.

512 cal · 93 cal/oz (stated)boil + 10 min cozy

Per serving: instant refried beans · tomato powder · TVP · spices · cheese (own bag) · Fritos-class chips from the lunch supply · ~12 oz water (page allows up to 16 for easier cleanup). Recipe weight 5.5 oz stated. Shake the bag before pouring — spices settle (stated).

Grown-up ramen: soba, shiitake, jerky, soy-sesame-sriracha. Mushrooms and buckwheat are a gamble with picky eaters; adults will be delighted.

🍑 Moroccan Chicken Couscous leader treat

Apricots, almonds, ras el hanout — the highest stated protein of any dinner in this book, and exactly the dinner a picky eater trades away.

665 cal · 40 g protein (stated)5 oz boil · zero simmer

2 servings: ½ cup couscous · 1 cup dried apricots, chopped · ¼ cup sliced almonds · 1 tsp sea salt · 4 tsp ras el hanout · 2 olive-oil packets (~2 Tbsp) · 1 7-oz chicken pouch · 1 True Lemon packet · 5 oz water.

🫒 Chicken Marbella leader treat

The Silver Palate classic, trail-built: prunes, olives, brown sugar, oregano. Prunes and olives is a two-strike count with picky eaters — it's genuinely delicious for the adults.

545 cal (stated)4 oz boil · zero simmer

2 servings: ½ cup couscous · ½ cup dried prunes, chopped · 1 Tbsp dried oregano · 1 Tbsp brown sugar · ½ tsp salt · ¼ tsp garlic powder · 1 broth packet · 1 Tbsp olive oil · 1 7-oz chicken pouch · 1 2.5-oz pouch pitted green olives · 2 vinegar packets (optional).

Breakfasts

Seven builds. The oatmeal base and the granola bag are the workhorses — one boil (or none) feeds the whole patrol. The simmered oatmeals are real porridge but cost real fuel.

🥣 The Milk-and-Sugar Oatmeal Base — +5 variations top shelf

The exact-ratio oatmeal build: bag at home, boiling water in camp, one-minute stand, zero cleanup. One 1650 ml boil (~5 cups) feeds five bags at once.

cal not statedboil-only · 1 min standbag or cup = bowl
Base, per serving
  • ½ cup quick-cooking oats
  • 4 Tbsp milk powder
  • Pinch of salt · 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 cup boiling water
Crowd-pick variations (as published)
  • Apple pie: freeze-dried apple + pinch cinnamon + walnuts + brown sugar
  • Pumpkin spice: 1 Tbsp powdered pumpkin + ½ tsp pumpkin-pie spice + brown sugar
In camp
  1. Boiling water into the cup or bag holding the mix.
  2. Stir, cover, stand 1 minute. Done.
Scaling

Pre-bag 2 base bags per person per morning and let them raid the trail-mix bag for toppings. (The page's miso and matcha variations exist; they are not for this audience.)

Source: trail.recipes.

🥛 Pecan Maple Granola in a Bag top shelf

Bake it at home, pre-load the bags with milk powder, and breakfast requires no stove at all — the fastest morning in the book, made for the pack-up-and-hike-out day.

580 cal/serving (stated)zero fuel possiblezero dishes
Makes 2 servings
  • ¾ cup rolled oats · ½ cup raw pecans, chopped
  • ¼ cup each unsweetened coconut, dried cranberries, maple syrup
  • 1 Tbsp oil (grapeseed/coconut) · ⅛ tsp salt
  • Plus 1 Tbsp powdered milk per serving bag
At home
  1. Mix all but the milk powder; bake compactly at 325°F, 20–25 min (rotate once, don't stir).
  2. Cool, break into clumps, split into two bags with 1 Tbsp milk powder each.
In camp
  1. ~⅓ cup (3 oz) water per bag, stir, eat. Warm water dissolves some milk powders better.

Quadruple the bake for the group — or use store-bought granola with the same stated ratio (1 Tbsp milk powder + 3 oz water per serving; that shortcut is ours). Source: Fresh Off The Grid.

No. 04

The Lid-Pan

Six recipes for the 5.5-inch titanium lid. The physics (No. 08) favors dense, flat food — which is why quesadillas and trout are top shelf and pancakes are a party trick.

🫓 Pepperoni & Cheese Quesadilla top shelf

Pizza flavors in quesadilla form — the highest payoff-per-gram frypan meal there is, from a published recipe written for exactly this style of cooking.

Serves 1455 cal (stated)~10 min~2–3 min flame each
Per person
  • 1 flour tortilla — street-taco size (4.5–5") so it lies flat on the lid
  • ~50 g grated cheddar (a generous handful)
  • 8 slices shelf-stable pepperoni
  • 1 Tbsp tubed tomato paste + 1 tsp dried oregano + 2 Tbsp water
  • 1 tsp olive oil
In camp
  1. Stir paste, oregano, and water in a mug — that's the sauce.
  2. Warm the oil on medium, drop to low, lay in the tortilla.
  3. Underside toasted → flip, paint on sauce, scatter cheese, let it melt.
  4. Add pepperoni, fold in half, slide out, cut into wedges.
At home

Pre-grate the cheese into a zip bag — saves carrying a knife for it.

Keep every scrap of cheese inside the fold — cheese on bare titanium carbonizes on a hot spot. Run it assembly-line, one per person. Pan wipes clean if nothing escaped. Source: trail.recipes.

🍕 Tortilla Trail Pizza top shelf

Hot pizza on day 2 of a wilderness trip is the biggest morale trick per ounce of effort — and the published recipe assumes a 6-inch pan. That's this lid.

1 tortilla = 1 pizzacal not stated~3–5 min each
Per pizza
  • 1 six-inch flour tortilla
  • Sauce: Boboli 5-oz pouch (~4–5 pizzas per pouch, our ratio) — or the tomato-paste sauce from the quesadilla
  • ~1 oz mozzarella or cheddar (our ratio)
  • Shelf-stable toppings: pepperoni, olives, jalapeños, bacon bits, red pepper flakes, parmesan
In camp
  1. Build the pizza cold: thin sauce layer, then cheese, then toppings.
  2. Set in the lightly oiled pan on medium-low (start lower than the recipe says — this is titanium).
  3. Tent foil or a lid over it so the cheese melts before the bottom scorches.
  4. Circle the pan slowly over the burner until golden underneath.

Failure mode is a burnt bottom under unmelted cheese — thin sauce, the foil tent, and constant pan movement are the three counters. Wipe the pan between pizzas; sauce drips carbonize. Source: Cloudline (item quantities where it leaves them open are our build).

🐟 Pan-Fried Trout — Seasoned Dredge, Two Ways top shelf

The meal the trip is half-built around. Dense fish is the documented titanium success story — pick a dredge, mix it at home, and fry in shifts.

Dredge A serves 3–4 troutcal not stated~10 min flame per fish14+ needs an Idaho license
Dredge A — seasoned flour
  • ½ cup flour
  • 1 tsp salt · ½ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp oregano · ¼ tsp cayenne
  • Olive oil — ~1 Tbsp per fish (our scaling for this pan)
Dredge B — cornmeal
  • ½ cup cornmeal
  • Salt + pepper (garlic powder optional)
  • Butter for the pan
In camp
  1. Gut, rinse, and pat the fish dry — dry skin is the difference between crisp and stuck.
  2. Whole fish: slash 3–4 shallow diagonal cuts per side so it cooks evenly.
  3. Shake the fish in the dredge bag, tap off excess.
  4. Lay into hot (not smoking) oil — ~4–5 min per side, turning once.
  5. Done when the flesh flakes and lifts off the bone. Finish with a True Lemon packet.
At home

Shake the dry dredge together in a quart zip bag; oil in a small leakproof bottle.

A 5.5" pan fits one small brookie or a folded fillet — shifts, and the best fisherman eats last. Medium-low, pan always circling. The fishy wipe rag must go in the bear hang. Sources: campingrecipes.com · Game & Fish.

🥪 Camp Grilled Cheese solid

The comfort-food fallback when someone won't eat the planned dinner — and the best use for bagel halves late in the trip.

Serves 1cal not stated~4–6 min each
Per sandwich
  • 2 slices dense bread (sourdough travels best) or 1 bagel, halved — sized to the pan
  • 1.5–2 oz block cheddar, pre-sliced at home
  • ~1 Tbsp butter (squeeze bottle/ghee) or olive oil
In camp
  1. Butter the outside faces generously.
  2. Low flame from the start — bread scorches on titanium faster than tortillas.
  3. Toast, pressing lightly, until golden; flip; keep the pan circling the whole time.
  4. Bagels go cut-face down first.

Our build (no strong published backpacking version exists). Expect one sacrificial slice while dialing the flame. Bulkier than tortillas for the same calories — which is the only reason it isn't top shelf.

🥞 Silver-Dollar Pancakes (Kodiak) demo only

A two-minute magic trick for morale — NOT a breakfast plan. Batter on bare titanium is the documented failure case; this is the one technique that makes it survivable.

Night One: Fresh

Day 1 is only ~6 miles after the drive, so the first dinner can be real food. The method that makes it safe: freeze perishables solid at home so they act as their own ice block thawing through day 1, pack them dead-center in the pack away from the sun side, and eat them first. (USDA danger zone: 40–140°F, max ~2 hours for perishables — the freeze-and-center method keeps you out of it.)

🌶 Fresh-Veg Quesadillas for the Group top shelf

The group dinner: real vegetables and fresh cheese on the one night weight doesn't matter, using the technique the lid-pan already proved.

No. 08

Method, Fuel & Cleanup

The four techniques that make every recipe above work on a canister stove at 8,600 ft.

The pot cozy — the biggest fuel trick there is

Most simmer-style meals don't need a flame the whole time: bring the pot to a boil, cook 1–2 minutes, lid on, kill the stove, and wrap the pot in a jacket or beanie for 10 minutes. The residual heat finishes the cooking. It stretches canister fuel dramatically and makes it impossible to scorch dinner — on a trip where the fuel math is fixed, this is the difference between comfortable and rationing.

Titanium frying technique

The 5.5-inch lid-pan is thin titanium — testers have measured a 300°F temperature spread across a bare titanium pan over a canister stove. The counter-moves: a real slick of high-smoke-point oil, the flame as LOW as it goes, and physically sliding the pan around over the burner so different zones take turns above the flame. Dense foods (trout, quesadillas, grilled cheese) come out great; batters and eggs stick even on seasoned pans — that's why the pancake recipe below is flagged demo-only.

Cleanup is a bear rule, not a chore

Favor recipes that absorb every drop of water — no straining means no food-scented gray water near camp. For a cheesy pot, boil a splash of water in it, swish, and scatter it well away from camp and 200 ft from water. Every wrapper, scrap, and the wash-water strainings are food to a bear: everything smellable hangs at night, wrappers included.

Scaling for the group

The 1650 ml Toaks comfortably cooks 2–3 big portions of any one-pot recipe here. For six-plus eaters, run two stoves and two pots in parallel, or cook in shifts — never overfill a titanium pot past ~⅔, or the boil-over puts dinner in the dirt. The group carries three canister stoves, so two cook-groups plus a hot-drink station is the natural formation.
